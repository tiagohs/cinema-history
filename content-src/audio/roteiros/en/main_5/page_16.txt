## [00] Opening  (256 car., ~0.3 min)

Part zero five: From nineteen seventy to nineteen eighty-nine. Chapter sixteen: The New Psychological Drama.

Psychological dramas took the spotlight in the nineteen seventies and nineteen eighties, with directors such as David Lynch and Bernardo Bertolucci.

## [01] House of Games  (1358 car., ~1.4 min)

Carl Jung was fascinated by films. "The cinema, like the detective story," he observed, "makes it possible to experience without danger all the excitement, passion and desirousness which must be repressed in a humanitarian ordering of life." This is precisely the role of the psychological drama: to explore the inner worlds of the characters (and, by association, of the viewers).

Sometimes they gain understanding - House of Games, from nineteen eighty-seven. In Sous le soleil de Satan (Under the Sun of Satan, nineteen eighty-seven), for example, they are destroyed. In Sleuth, from nineteen seventy-two, the hero's curiosity, greed and envy bring about his downfall. Jeffrey's odyssey through the terrifying world of Blue Velvet, from nineteen eighty-six, feeds his own fascination: he is both attracted and repelled. This makes him at once protagonist and antagonist - and, in doing so, confirms that humanity really is its own worst enemy.

As Freud suggests, to understand ourselves we must confront our secret (and repressed) desires. Bernardo Bertolucci (born in nineteen forty) drew on his fantasies about anonymous sex with a beautiful woman to create Ultimo tango a Parigi (Last Tango in Paris, nineteen seventy-two). The result is a meditation on grief, sexual violence and fantasy; the director gave outward form to his secret, unspoken thoughts.

## [02] Il portiere di notte  (1222 car., ~1.2 min)

In Il portiere di notte (The Night Porter, nineteen seventy-four), director Liliana Cavani (born in nineteen thirty-three) questions power, choice, guilt and sexual desire. A former SS officer is confronted with a figure from his past - a woman with whom he had a sadomasochistic affair while she was imprisoned in a concentration camp. For Freud, this is the very essence of repression - a secret history of sexual encounters and the ongoing struggle to deny or refute them. The Night Porter exposes the characters' deepest desires and asks the audience to confront their own.

In his theory of the "mirror stage" of human development, psychoanalyst Jacques Lacan examined the role of the mirror in allowing a small child to think of itself as an individual ("I") separate from its mother and the rest of the world. In films, the concept of the mirror image can be expressed metaphorically, by exploring the way antagonist and protagonist define each other. In Manhunter, from nineteen eighty-six, the hero gains crucial insight into the minds of killers by being able to think like them.

## [03] Frenzy  (1416 car., ~1.4 min)

Hero and villain are like two halves - opposite but linked, like Blaney and Rusk in Frenzy, from nineteen seventy-two, by Alfred Hitchcock (eighteen ninety-nine to nineteen eighty). In Blade Runner, from nineteen eighty-two, the protagonist Deckard understands the conflict of the antagonist, Batty. If we assume that Deckard, like Batty, is a replicant, they are, in effect, brothers. David Cronenberg (born in nineteen forty-three) took this to the extreme in Dead Ringers, from nineteen eighty-eight, in which twin brothers share each other's lives - one whole split in two - and one cannot exist without the other.

Psychological dramas plunge into the darkest places of the mind. Spoorloos (The Vanishing, nineteen eighty-eight) is a terrifying study of the psyche of a killer, a family man who has lived a "good life" but whose inner and outer personalities are very different. In Don't Look Now, from nineteen seventy-three, a grieving couple travels to Venice shortly after the death of their daughter. The husband - who may have psychic gifts - is tormented by glimpses of a figure in a red coat, which reminds him of his daughter. The audience's understanding of events remains as limited and faltering as that of the husband and wife in their grief. In the words of David Mamet: "Viewers understand that they are the protagonists, and indeed, for one night they truly are, because the drama is their dream."
