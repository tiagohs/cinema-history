Part zero five: From nineteen seventy to nineteen eighty-nine. Chapter six: Paranoia and Conspiracy Theories in Hollywood.

The nineteen seventies were troubled, paranoid years, and Hollywood couldn't help but mirror that on screen.

## [01] Watergate scandal  (1645 car., ~1.6 min)

The production of political and psychological thrillers in nineteen seventies Hollywood is generally interpreted as a paranoid reaction to the Watergate scandal and a residue of the witch hunt against communists in the nineteen fifties. Other contributing factors include the specter of the Vietnam War and the feeling among liberals that, although everyone hated the war, it went on because of the government's stubbornness.

There was a growing sense that Hollywood did not understand its audience. All of this combined left the film world insecure and the nation introspective. The prototype of Hollywood conspiracy films is The Manchurian Candidate, from nineteen sixty-two, directed by John Frankenheimer (nineteen twenty to two thousand two). While seemingly about a communist plot against the American government, the film reveals that the conspiracy is the work of homegrown politicians and military leaders. During the nineteen sixties and nineteen seventies, films portraying the authorities as ambiguous and corrupt became common.

However, Hollywood's paranoia peaked with the Watergate scandal in nineteen seventy-two. A definite sense of unease became visible in many Hollywood genres: in Westerns such as Pat Garrett and Billy the Kid, from nineteen seventy-three, political dramas such as The Candidate, from nineteen seventy-two, gangster films such as The Godfather Part Two, from nineteen seventy-four, and science fiction works such as Soylent Green, from nineteen seventy-three. In each of them, the forces of American power and politics are portrayed as untrustworthy, exploiting ordinary people for personal or corporate gain.

## [02] The Parallax View  (1433 car., ~1.4 min)

Soylent Green presents the darkest vision, portraying the New York of twenty seventeen as a dystopia in which people unwittingly live as cannibals and corrupt officials help cover up the truth. The iconic film of the period is The Parallax View, from nineteen seventy-four, a dark thriller directed by Alan J. Pakula (nineteen twenty-eight to nineteen ninety-eight) in which the hippie reporter played by Warren Beatty (born in nineteen thirty-seven) investigates the Kennedy-style assassination of a committed American senator and stumbles upon a plot that reaches the highest echelons of business and government.

The film set the stylistic template for the conspiracy film: labyrinthine narrative, booming sound effects, overlapping dialogue, brutal steel-and-glass architecture, and sparing use of music on the soundtrack. The Conversation, from nineteen seventy-four, directed by Francis Ford Coppola (born in nineteen thirty-nine), and Night Moves, from nineteen seventy-five, by Arthur Penn (nineteen twenty-two to twenty ten), adopt an equally cold, modernist style but tell more intimate stories. Both films star Gene Hackman as a private investigator who confronts forces far beyond his control and loses his identity in the process.

## [03] All the President's Men  (910 car., ~0.9 min)

In these films, the doomed hero, though intelligent and committed, becomes a victim not only of powerful outside influences but also of his own emotional weakness, his instability, and his obsessive nature.

The only film to deal directly with the Watergate scandal was All the President's Men, from nineteen seventy-six. Despite its narrative complexity and fact-packed screenplay, the film was a hit with an audience desperate to leave the Nixon era behind. After it, with Nixon defeated and the Vietnam War over, paranoia began to give way to the optimism of films such as Star Wars, from nineteen seventy-seven. Traces remained in works such as Marathon Man, from nineteen seventy-six, directed by John Schlesinger (nineteen twenty-six to two thousand three), although the corrupting force in that film belongs not to a rotten government organization but to an individual, in the form of a Nazi war criminal.
