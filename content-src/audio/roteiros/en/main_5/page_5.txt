## [01] Blaxploitation  (1663 car., ~1.7 min)

Hollywood is white. It is a place of white men making films with white people for audiences of all colors to watch. Diversity is still not a priority in American productions. Black actresses and actors, when they are on screen, often end up in stereotyped and pejorative roles. Although there are some exceptions, such as the cast chosen for the live-action adaptation of The Lion King, from twenty nineteen, it is important to understand that the problem still seems far from being solved

A twenty sixteen study by the University of Southern California examined the eight hundred highest-grossing films from two thousand seven to twenty fifteen, analyzing thirty-five thousand two hundred five characters, and found that seventy-three point seven percent were white. And only five point five percent of the eight hundred eighty-six directors were Black. Even so, it is important to stress that, though still far from ideal, the Black movement has been gaining strength in cinema in a gradual and healthy way.

[CITAÇÃO] You knew we had something really exciting, but we had no idea how big it would be.

said Richard Roundtree on Shaft, nineteen seventy-one.

This progress is the fruit of exhausting work, built through hard struggle and much sweat by generations of Black filmmakers fighting for a more diverse cinema. One of the most emblematic milestones of this struggle emerged in the early nineteen seventies, when great social and political revolutions were erupting throughout the culture industry. In this period, remembered as one of the most turbulent and important in recent history, a film movement emerged that finally gave a voice to Black people: Blaxploitation.

## [02] Part 2  (796 car., ~0.8 min)

The sarcastic term was coined by former producer and former president of the Los Angeles National Association of Colored People (NAACP), Junius Griffin, and arose from a blend of the words black and exploitation. Blaxploitation, or Blacksploitation, was an ethnic movement that placed Black artists as the protagonists of audiovisual productions.

They were films made by Black filmmakers, with Black actresses and actors, for Black audiences to watch and feel represented. Blaxploitation features were mainly set in poor urban neighborhoods and often dealt with themes such as slavery and miscegenation. More than revolutionizing cinema by placing previously peripheral characters at the center of the plot, these films provoked the status quo and poked fun at the usual archetypes of white films.

## [03] Shaft  (754 car., ~0.8 min)

One of the first and most representative works of Blaxploitation, Shaft, from nineteen seventy-one, for example, is set in Harlem, a peripheral neighborhood of New York, and follows a Black detective who gets involved with the Italian mafia to find the missing daughter of a Black gangster. The film addressed the Black Power movement and issues of race, masculinity, and sexuality. Based on a book written by Ernest Tidyman, whose protagonist was originally white, the film had its entire plot significantly altered simply by the casting of actor Richard Roundtree (born in nineteen forty-two) as John Shaft. Costing five hundred thousand dollars, the feature grossed thirteen million and showed the traditional studios that this was a promising market.

## [04] Learn more: The Ultimate Embodiment of a Movement  (2255 car., ~2.2 min)

"He's a cool, tough guy. A Black private detective, a sex machine with all the ladies. He takes orders from nobody, Black or white, but he'd risk his own neck for his brother. I'm talking about Shaft. Got it?" These lines, from the theme song by Oscar winner Isaac Hayes (nineteen forty-two to two thousand eight), are the perfect introduction to the Black American hero/rebel idol John Shaft, played by Richard Roundtree, the title star of the hugely successful feature directed by Gordon Parks. Shaft followed the path opened by Melvin Van Peebles's Sweet Sweetback's Baadasssss Song, from nineteen seventy-one, and is widely recognized as the film that launched the short-lived (but fondly remembered) explosion of blaxploitation cinema.

The screenplay was written by Ernest Tidyman (nineteen twenty-eight to nineteen eighty-four), author of a popular series of detective stories starring the film's protagonist. After the success of Sweetback, MGM gave Parks - a Black American and a prestigious writer, composer, photographer and film director - permission to make a project aimed at tapping into the emerging Black audience market. Parks wanted a new face for the lead role and found exactly what he was looking for in Roundtree, a former Ebony model and occasional stage actor whose physical presence and acting style had the exact combination of machismo, virility and confidence the role required.

If ever there was a film in which the narrative is simply a vehicle for showcasing a character, it is Shaft. Together, Tidyman, Parks and Roundtree created a strong Black protagonist who - for the first time in Hollywood films - makes his own rules, listens to no one, gives orders instead of taking them and has no qualms about making jokes at the expense of white authorities. Despite (or possibly because of) its subversive and militant undertones, Shaft was extremely profitable among both Black and white audiences, grossing more than twenty-three million dollars in the United States alone. Such broad success can be partly explained by the fact that Shaft is perfectly at ease in any situation, with all kinds of people, and that his magnetism and his cool under fire transcend any color line.

## [05] Samuel L. Jackson  (1534 car., ~1.5 min)

The film got a follow-up in the two thousands, starring Samuel L. Jackson (born in nineteen forty-eight), which tells the story of the original Shaft's nephew, a cop trying to arrest a racist killer and drug dealer who uses his money to keep getting away with it.

Considered the first Black action hero, Roundtree, alongside Pam Grier (born in nineteen forty-nine) and Jim Kelly (nineteen forty-six to twenty thirteen), belongs to the group of actors who stood out most in Blaxploitation. He became an icon of the culture industry, later appearing in series such as ninety thousand two hundred ten and Desperate Housewives, and in the film Se7en, from nineteen ninety-five, by David Fincher (born in nineteen sixty-two).

said Melvin Van Peebles.

The troubled lives of the artists as activists for the Black cause blended with the work they were helping to produce. The star of Super Fly, from nineteen seventy-two, Ron O’Neal (nineteen thirty-seven to two thousand four), for example, grew up in a poor neighborhood of Cleveland, Ohio. He lost his father and brother at sixteen. During his life, he worked with the oldest African American theater company in the United States. Success on stage took him to New York, where he taught acting classes in Harlem at the Youth Arts Program, an institution that offers artistic training and provides financial aid scholarships to low-income students.

## [06] Curtis Mayfield  (1098 car., ~1.1 min)

It was during this period that he was invited to take part in Super Fly. The feature follows a drug dealer who wants to get out of the world of crime and, to achieve his goal, needs to pull off one last job. An instant success, the film caused great controversy over the way its characters are portrayed, adding even more fuel to the debate about the role of Blaxploitation. The Congress of Racial Equality, the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People, and other organizations tried to block its distribution. The Student National Coordinating Committee also protested against the film as a tool of white oppression.

However, despite all the controversy, what stood out most in the film was the soundtrack produced by singer-songwriter Curtis Mayfield (nineteen forty-two to nineteen ninety-nine), which quickly became a classic of American cinema and is remembered to this day as one of the best of all time. Blaxploitation soundtracks were almost all composed by renowned Black musicians of the American industry, such as Quincy Jones, Barry White, Marvin Gaye, and James Brown.

## [07] Blacula  (1301 car., ~1.3 min)

Funk and jazz were always present, establishing themselves as a significant part of the movement. The recognition was so great that the theme from Shaft won the Oscar for Best Original Song, establishing composer Isaac Hayes as the first Black person in history to win an Oscar in a non-acting category. It is estimated that by nineteen seventy-six approximately two hundred films had been produced. The subgenres were quite diverse, covering crime stories, martial arts, prison, comedy, nostalgia, and drama, all the way to musical Westerns and horror.

Yet the plot almost always brought to the surface something intrinsic to the movement. In Blacula, from nineteen seventy-two, Count Dracula bites an African prince and locks him in a coffin. The prince manages to free himself centuries later and takes on a new name: Blacula. While quenching his thirst for blood (and killing several people along the way), he meets a woman who is the reincarnation of his late wife. The film is one of the most emblematic and characteristic horror features of Blaxploitation. The irony used to unmask prejudice was put into practice by satirizing the films that were successful at the time. The heroes (or antiheroes) have strong personalities, are attractive, and fight against the ills and injustices of society.

## [08] Sweet Sweetback's Baadasssss Song  (1726 car., ~1.7 min)

The villains are, for the most part, white police officers who carry out this oppression. The result could not have been otherwise. The public became divided over the genre. Sweet Sweetback's Baadasssss Song, from nineteen seventy-one, for example, tells the story of Sweetback, a male prostitute who helps a stranger escape the clutches of two racist cops. The film had several scenes censored, and some critics considered it sensationalist. Some of the groups that pushed for the end of the movement were even made up of Black people who accused the films of reinforcing negative stereotypes. Junius Griffin declared that they should keep their children from being exposed to these works, which glorified "Black males as dope pushers, pimps, gangsters, and super males."

Thanks to the controversies that fueled the differences of opinion, the movement's popularity waned until it came to an end. The popularity of these works inspired other genres to create Black characters with similar traits. Some famous examples are Live and Let Die, from nineteen seventy-three, and Enter the Dragon, from nineteen seventy-three, with Bruce Lee (nineteen forty to nineteen seventy-three). The rise of Black artists in the nineteen eighties, such as Eddie Murphy (born in nineteen sixty-one), Denzel Washington (born in nineteen fifty-four), Samuel L. Jackson, Spike Lee (born in nineteen fifty-seven), and Halle Berry (born in nineteen sixty-six), reflects the indirect impact that Blaxploitation had.

Quentin Tarantino, an outspoken fan of Blaxploitation, paid homage to the movement in his third feature. The director brought Pam Grier (born in nineteen forty-nine) out of obscurity to star in Jackie Brown, from nineteen ninety-seven.

## [09] Learn more: One of the Pioneers of Blaxploitation (1)  (1483 car., ~1.5 min)

"This film is dedicated to all the brothers and sisters who have had enough of the Man." With the seventy thousand dollars he earned for his nineteen seventy comedy Watermelon Man, from nineteen seventy, plus additional funds (including a loan from Bill Cosby), Melvin Van Peebles (born in nineteen thirty-two) - one of the first Black American directors to work in Hollywood - financed his new project, Sweet Sweetback's Baadasssss Song. He wrote, directed, scored and starred in the film, which was a wise economic decision and also ensured his total creative control over the production. In early nineteen seventy-one, Sweetback opened in the only two theaters willing to show it. By the end of that year, the film had become the most profitable independent production in history. A hit across the United States, it grossed more than fifteen million dollars.

Sweetback is so original in concept and execution that it defied all expectations, offering a popular alternative to the dominant Hollywood paradigm. But it also borrows narrative themes and conventions from a variety of genres, including gangster chase films, biker films and soft-core porn. Ultimately, Sweetback is a film whose unexpected commercial success secured it a place at the forefront of an explosion of productions aimed at the Black market, with Black casts and/or Black directors - a movement that soon became known by the ambivalent name of blaxploitation.

## [10] Learn more: One of the Pioneers of Blaxploitation (2)  (1501 car., ~1.5 min)

The shocking opening scene shows us a preteen Sweetback (played by Mario, Melvin's son) working in a brothel, where a grateful prostitute cries out his nickname during an orgasm. We then see an adult Sweetback performing as a porn actor in a sex show in South Central, Los Angeles. After seeing two white police officers beat a young Black activist (Hubert Scales) nearly to death, Sweetback attacks the officers and almost kills them. The rest of the film follows the hero's misadventures: he flees and hitches rides in a desperate effort to avoid capture. At one point, Sweetback's life is threatened by a biker gang, and he survives only by winning a public sexual duel with its female leader. As one reviewer put it, Sweetback "escapes the police by raping a Black woman at knifepoint, impales a policeman with a pool cue, kills several dogs that are following him, heals himself with his own urine and bites the head off a lizard before fleeing across the Mexican border into the desert." The film ends with the words "A 'Baadasssss' Black man is coming back to collect some dues" flashing across the screen.

Whatever one concludes about Sweetback's sociopolitical message, the energy and innovative directing style of Van Peebles can hardly be denied. Making creative use of techniques such as montage, superimposition, freeze frames, jump cuts, zoom-ins, split screen, stylized dialogue, multiple exposure and a great soundtrack, Van Peebles broke new ground and challenged viewers of all races.

## [11] Race movies  (831 car., ~0.8 min)

Despite the criticism, many of the sparks of representation seen in cinema today are the result of the revolutions these revolutionary movements brought about. From Hattie McDaniel (eighteen ninety-three to nineteen fifty-two), the first Black actress to win an Oscar (who received the award at a hotel that did not allow Black people inside), to the rise of race movies, the success of Sidney Poitier (born in nineteen twenty-seven), and even the recent Oscars So White protest. It is impossible to measure the quality of the impact that Blaxploitation had on American cinema. Some argue that it was significant and necessary for diversity in Hollywood works. Others question the quality and relevance of this inclusion. Nevertheless, even though it ended decades ago, the movement's lessons and legacy still have an impact today.
