## [01] Star Wars  (913 car., ~0.9 min)

With Star Wars, from nineteen seventy-seven, and Superman, from nineteen seventy-eight, audience interest in fantasy and science fiction films grew considerably, with Superman being the first major big-budget superhero film, performing well at the box office and with critics. The nineteen eighties brought some successful adaptations, with RoboCop, from nineteen eighty-seven, and Batman, from nineteen eighty-nine, directed by Tim Burton (born in nineteen fifty-eight), as the biggest highlights.

Other films were released during the nineteen eighties and nineteen nineties, including four Superman sequels and another three Batman films, but few achieved the success of their predecessors. Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles, from nineteen ninety, despite mixed reviews and skepticism about investing in people dressed in rubber suits, was a hit and became the highest-grossing independent production up to that time.

Learn more: The Watershed in Superhero Films.

In the early two thousands, the first Spider-Man was released in theaters, proving once and for all that films based on flamboyant comic book superheroes could work on the big screen. It is true that, two years earlier, the first X-Men had been released, receiving good reviews and a hefty box office. However, Bryan Singer's X-Men, good as it was, was still a work fairly removed from comic book aesthetics, without vibrant colors and colorful costumes.

The fear at the time was that this would not go over well with audiences, even considering the Superman films decades earlier. Spider-Man is a hero who, however much one might want to change his origin story and subsequent events, had to keep something crucial to his mystique: the costume. And we have to admit that the colors red and blue on a skintight suit covering the whole body, with web patterns and spiders on the chest and back, had every chance of not working on film. However, changing the costume, as Singer elegantly did in his X-Men, would have meant that fans (and Spider-Man has many, many generations of them) would stage an unprecedented revolt. It was no accident, then, that Spider-Man remained in so-called development hell for no less than twenty-five years.

## [03] Learn more: The Watershed in Superhero Films (2)  (1544 car., ~1.5 min)

Only when Sony, in nineteen ninety-nine, bought the film adaptation rights from MGM, which had already commissioned a draft screenplay from James Cameron (born in nineteen fifty-four), did the film version of your friendly neighborhood Spider-Man really take off. Cameron was no longer attached to the project, but his work served as the basis for the screenplay that David Koepp (born in nineteen sixty-three), writer of Mission: Impossible, from nineteen ninety-six, and Jurassic Park, from nineteen ninety-three, among many other famous films, ended up developing. Several directors were considered to helm the film, including Chris Columbus (born in nineteen fifty-eight) and David Fincher (born in nineteen sixty-two). But it was when Sam Raimi joined the project that it began to take definitive shape. Raimi was firm about the film's structure, settling on just one villain, the Green Goblin, whereas the original idea was to use Doctor Octopus as well, in a secondary role. Likewise, with Raimi, the most dramatic change to the character's mythology would be made: the traditional mechanical web-shooters were replaced by controversial organic web-shooters, explained as part of the mutation Peter Parker undergoes after being bitten by a genetically altered spider. Spider-Man's suit was kept as close as possible to the original from the comics. It was given a rubbery look and raised webbing, and was molded to the actor's body almost as a single piece. Thus, the transposition from the pages of the comics to the screen was perfect.

## [04] Learn more: The Watershed in Superhero Films (3)  (2362 car., ~2.4 min)

Raimi, at the time best known for his work on the Evil Dead series, but who had also already made one of the best superhero films (though not one based on comics), Darkman, from nineteen ninety, was the perfect choice. His screenplay decisions were crucial to the film's success, and in addition he worked with a vibrant color palette, heavy on red, orange, and yellow, to keep the hero's and villain's costumes from standing out too much from their surroundings and also to mimic the multicolored comics of the House of Ideas. The result, for better or worse, was that the whole picture ended up with a strongly cartoonish feel, almost literally torn from the material that inspired it. The finishing touch was the essential work of veteran John Dykstra (born in nineteen forty-seven), famous for the special effects of Star Wars. The digital construction of the arachnid's movements may seem dated today, but that is only because technology advances at a maddening pace. The fact remains, however, that he managed to bring the hero's incredible leaps and acrobatics to the screen very effectively.

Everything works very organically in the film, even though, of course, the time Raimi needs to tell the character's origin ends up taking away much of the picture's action potential. But the truth is that Raimi, along with Richard Donner (born in nineteen thirty) in nineteen seventy-eight and, more recently, Jon Favreau (born in nineteen sixty-six) in Iron Man, tells one of the best superhero "origin" stories, without ever letting the pace flag. It is a very well-balanced mix of respect for the original work, skillful adaptation, and balance with the rest of the film. Despite the bland performances of Dunst and Franco, Tobey Maguire's (born in nineteen seventy-five) Peter Parker, part goofball, part nerd, part beloved nephew, but entirely hero, has a perfectly acceptable arc throughout the film. Willem Dafoe (born in nineteen fifty-five) plays the comics' cartoonish villain just as the role demands, serving as a counterpoint to Parker's more serene attitude. The production design's sin lies in the, shall we say, Power Rangers-style armor the Goblin was given, but it does not compromise the result. Spider-Man remains one of the best examples of a great superhero film. At the very least, the film deserves its status as a watershed for the genre.

## [05] The Crow  (1322 car., ~1.3 min)

The Crow, from nineteen ninety-four, directed by Alex Proyas (born in nineteen sixty-three), ended up becoming a pioneer in the evolution of the adaptations that would follow its release, with a darker and more violent story aimed at a more mature audience. The success of The Crow spurred the release of a film version of Spawn, from nineteen ninety-seven, the flagship character of Image Comics. The success of Image Comics' "darker" characters changed the direction of comic book films.

Marvel, with Men in Black, from nineteen ninety-seven, and Blade, from nineteen ninety-eight, aimed to develop profitable franchises. With the purchase of Malibu Comics (owner of The Men in Black comics), Marvel and Columbia Pictures set about adapting the comic in nineteen ninety-seven, with Steven Spielberg (born in nineteen forty-six) as executive producer. The film became the first adaptation of a Marvel property to win an Oscar, a feat that would only be repeated in two thousand two, with Spider-Man, from two thousand two, directed by Sam Raimi (born in nineteen fifty-nine).

The Matrix, from nineteen ninety-nine, had a gigantic impact on superhero films. Influenced by comic books, cyberpunk science fiction, anime, and kung fu films, The Matrix paved the way for a new era of superhero films in the twenty-first century.

## [06] X-Men  (1631 car., ~1.6 min)

After this comic book boom, the first decade of the twenty-first century brought even greater interest in superhero films. The success of the X-Men TV series led Twentieth Century Fox to license the film rights in nineteen ninety-four. After the success of Men in Black in nineteen ninety-seven, Columbia Pictures licensed the rights to Spider-Man in nineteen ninety-nine.

The century would begin with X-Men, from two thousand, directed by Bryan Singer (born in nineteen sixty-five), considered a milestone in the subgenre. The film was a tremendous box office success, spawning a long film franchise, and proved once and for all that audiences were hungry for new comic book adaptations. That same year, M. Night Shyamalan (born in nineteen seventy) released Unbreakable, from two thousand. Going against the grain of fantastical superhero films, Shyamalan focused on urban elements and naturalism.

In two thousand two, the Spider-Man phenomenon hit theaters, breaking box office and DVD sales records. Its success followed a long list of uneven adaptations of Marvel and DC heroes, such as Daredevil, from two thousand three, Hulk, from two thousand three, Catwoman, from two thousand four, Hellboy, from two thousand four, and Fantastic Four, from two thousand five. Some critics and experts attributed the rising popularity of superhero franchises to the social and political climate in Western society since the terrorist attacks of September eleventh, two thousand one, while others argued that advances in special effects technology played a more significant role.

## [07] Learn more: The Definitive Batman (and Film)  (1853 car., ~1.9 min)

Learn more: The Definitive Batman (and Film).

Even with all the success enjoyed by the competition, few films have reached the excellence of The Dark Knight, in quality and in numbers. It was the first comic book hero adaptation to break the one billion dollars barrier at the box office. It earned an Oscar for one of its leads, Heath Ledger, who delivered the best version of the Joker in any medium. It established a tone and a narrative style that brought fantasy characters into the "real world" in a believable way. Perhaps only Logan, from twenty seventeen, among screen superhero films, has achieved such thematic depth and such dramatic density.

Nolan obviously had no interest whatsoever in getting his hands on other heroes from the DC catalog. The idea of a "shared universe," of using one film as just another brick to complete a wall, never crossed his mind. His trilogy made it clear that his interest was, rather, in creating an allegory about the darkest corners of the human mind, such as fear, obsession, revenge, and madness. Batman and the characters orbiting his adventures proved to be the perfect starting point for exploring very real themes. A perfect approach, which turned the director's second film with the Caped Crusader into a worldwide phenomenon, legitimizing "superhero movies" as a force to be reckoned with beyond box office numbers.

The Dark Knight proved, a decade ago, that the answer lies not in imitating a model but in trusting one's artists. When Patty Jenkins worked with little interference, she delivered Wonder Woman, from twenty seventeen, and the world applauded. Perhaps that is the best legacy of the adventure that pitted Christian Bale (born in nineteen seventy-four) and Heath Ledger against each other in a duel that was less physical and more psychological: it is never too late to learn new tricks.

## [08] Batman Begins  (1053 car., ~1.1 min)

But it was only in two thousand five, with the release of Batman Begins, from two thousand five, directed by Christopher Nolan (born in nineteen seventy), that adaptations maintained and expanded on the quality that Spider-Man had shown three years earlier. Its sequel, The Dark Knight, from two thousand eight, is considered one of the best superhero films ever released, earning eight Oscar nominations and winning the Oscar for Best Supporting Actor for Heath Ledger (nineteen seventy-nine to two thousand eight), something unprecedented at the time.

In November two thousand five, Marvel regained the film rights to Iron Man from New Line Cinema, and in two thousand eight it released its first film through Marvel Studios, Iron Man, from two thousand eight, marking the beginning of what would become known as the Marvel Cinematic Universe. It was an undertaking that would result in more than twenty-three interconnected films, becoming the most profitable franchise in film history, with a total of more than twenty-one billion dollars in revenue.
