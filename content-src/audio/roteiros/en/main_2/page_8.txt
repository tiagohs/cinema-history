Part zero two: From nineteen thirty to nineteen thirty-nine. Chapter eight: Sound and Comedy.

When talkies replaced silent films in the late nineteen twenties, comedians took radical steps to adapt to the new possibilities, and new masters of comedy took the spotlight.

## [01] Laurel and Hardy  (1690 car., ~1.7 min)

The tradition of physical comedy and slapstick inaugurated by Mack Sennett (eighteen eighty to nineteen sixty) was transformed into a verbal choreography in which one actor played the straight man while the other reeled off the jokes.

Jimmy Durante (eighteen ninety-three to nineteen eighty), for example, loved to scramble the order of his sentences, and W. C. Fields (eighteen eighty to nineteen forty-six) - who improvised most of his own dialogue - constantly played with double meanings in films such as It's a Gift, from nineteen thirty-four.

Hearing comedians talk was a new and bizarre experience, and some of them were better suited to the new medium than others. Resisting the trend toward talkies, Charlie Chaplin (eighteen eighty-nine to nineteen seventy-seven) released the silent City Lights, from nineteen thirty-one; the filmmaker would not make The Great Dictator, from nineteen forty, his first film with dialogue, until nineteen forty. Stan Laurel (eighteen ninety to nineteen sixty-five) and Oliver Hardy (eighteen ninety-two to nineteen fifty-seven), the famous Laurel and Hardy, adapted immediately to the new era, but for other comedians the transition was not so easy.

Constrained by studio pressures and restrictions, Buster Keaton (eighteen ninety-five to nineteen sixty-six) found himself cut off from the artistic flourishes of his silent films and forced to settle for talkies such as Speak Easily, from nineteen thirty-two. Harold Lloyd's career went into decline and Harry Langdon soon fell into obscurity.

When it comes to comedy, Laurel and Hardy remain the prototype of the yin-yang duo. However, their opposing personas were far more subtle and malleable than mere fat/thin or clown/straight-man dichotomies. Laurel's hallmarks were his effeminate, contemplative delicacy and a profound idiocy that sometimes gave way to bizarre bursts of cleverness. Ollie, for his part, was clumsy and pompous, but he also longed to appear gallant, as shown by his habit of bashfully twiddling his tie.

Much of the duo's genius lay in their mastery of the formalities of absurd comedy: the leisurely slowness of the double takes that punctuated their routines; the way Ollie stared in frustration at the camera; and the carefully staged episodes of their constant war with regular foil James Finlayson (eighteen eighty-seven to nineteen fifty-three), in which the two would watch his explosions of rage before retaliating, always after exchanging a solemn nod.

## [03] Looney Tunes  (794 car., ~0.8 min)

With the addition of sound and recorded dialogue, comedies entered the realm of surrealism. Comic moments leaned toward the absurd, with Oliver Hardy's neck stretching impossibly in Way Out West, from nineteen thirty-seven, and Harpo Marx making a candle burn at both ends in Horse Feathers, from nineteen thirty-two. This kind of fantastic situation foreshadowed the illogical world of the Looney Tunes.

Many of the comedians of the nineteen thirties came from vaudeville and had honed their skills on stage before going into film. W. C. Fields's pool table routine in Six of a Kind, from nineteen thirty-four, was created during his years in the theater. Jack Benny was already a successful violinist and Jimmy Durante a jazz pianist - and both made use of those talents in their performances.

## [04] Way Out West  (916 car., ~0.9 min)

Drawing on the perfectionism honed in stage rehearsals, Stan Laurel and Oliver Hardy worked tirelessly on the two-minute dance sequence in Way Out West, from nineteen thirty-seven. Some of these comedians created peculiar personas, defined by unmistakable characteristics, popular characters or distinctive traits, such as Jimmy Durante's big nose or skinny Stan in contrast to chubby Ollie. The two reached their creative peak during the nineteen thirties, appearing in classics such as The Music Box, from nineteen thirty-two.

Perhaps the most brilliant comic creation of the period was the Marx Brothers. Of the three, Groucho was the most iconic figure, with his glasses, his cigar and his endless supply of biting wisecracks. The Marx Brothers brought their anarchic slapstick style from the stage to the screen, combining it with dialogue that had been too complex for the era when they performed in vaudeville.

## [05] The Three Stooges  (752 car., ~0.8 min)

In films such as Duck Soup, from nineteen thirty-three, they defined the art of sarcastic comedy, and their influence on comedians such as Woody Allen (born nineteen thirty-five) is clear in the film Love and Death, from nineteen seventy-five, for example.

While the Marx Brothers were defining comic cinema, The Three Stooges, their fellow New Yorkers, emerged and became the cornerstone of American popular comedy. Curly, Larry and Moe had an extraordinarily prolific career together, starring (with some later lineup changes) in more than two hundred shorts and feature films. The Three Stooges are best remembered for their slapstick style full of violence, often inflicted on one another, in films such as Dizzy Doctors, from nineteen thirty-seven.
