## [01] A Nightmare on Elm Street  (1057 car., ~1.1 min)

Keeping up the forty-year nostalgia cycle, the twenty tens saw the pendulum of pop culture swing toward the seventies and eighties, producing a wave of reboots and belated sequels. At best, these remakes are transparent cash grabs: without substance, but largely harmless, like A Nightmare on Elm Street, from twenty ten, or Piranha 3D, from twenty ten. But despite that, great examples emerged, such as Blade Runner twenty forty-nine, from twenty seventeen, Mad Max: Fury Road, from twenty fifteen, Evil Dead, from twenty thirteen, and True Grit, from twenty ten.

At worst, they spoil the cultural legacy of fondly remembered franchises; such was the case with Ghostbusters, from twenty sixteen. In any case, reboots, well received or not, are a safe bet for studios: there is an established, guaranteed audience, drawn by nostalgic curiosity if nothing else. Unfortunately, with the rise of these releases, novelty has become a financial gamble that studios are increasingly hesitant to take, perhaps only superhero movies are the exception in this regard.

## [06] Remake  (1353 car., ~1.4 min)

But what exactly is a remake, reboot, prequel, sequel or spin-off? Starting with the first term, a remake "redoes" the original material almost entirely, and may involve a modernization or reinterpretation, as we see in the versions of Scarface, from nineteen eighty-three, the original, by Howard Hawks (eighteen ninety-six to nineteen seventy-seven), is from nineteen thirty-two. The concept is based on repeating plot and characters. The new version can be more or less faithful to the original, with the addition of new elements, but without straying from the essence of the source film. The examples are countless: Let Me In, from twenty ten, RoboCop, from twenty fourteen, Carrie, from twenty thirteen.

Reboot, literally, basically means "to restart". It is a completely new version of a previously released film, disregarding the background presented before. It is a fresh start; the story and its characters are no longer connected to the previous work. While a remake basically repeats what has already been done, a reboot offers new narratives. Lucrative superhero movies have followed this "restart" trend. The most emblematic case is Spider-Man. In less than two decades, the hero has already had three reboots on the big screen.

## [07] Spin-off  (1374 car., ~1.4 min)

Any conversation about savvy marketing would be incomplete without mentioning the media monopoly in the room: Disney. Following the trend of low-financial-risk reboots, the Disney corporation began reimagining its classic films as live-action remakes: Cinderella, from twenty fifteen, Beauty and the Beast, from twenty seventeen, The Lion King, from twenty nineteen, Dumbo, from twenty nineteen, Aladdin, from twenty nineteen, and The Jungle Book, from twenty sixteen, are some of them. They change very little of the source material, except for self-referential additions or, now that a certain brand of palatable feminism has been deemed marketable, a more progressive bent.

A spin-off, on the other hand, is a kind of by-product, a film derived from another. What happens is that in some cases, after the audience's response, a supporting character "steals" the show and becomes so popular that it earns a production of its own. For example, the character Puss in Boots from Shrek two, from two thousand four, got his own film in twenty eleven, presenting his story without the other characters seen in Shrek, from two thousand one. Recently, Star Wars has been one of the biggest examples of spin-offs: Rogue One: A Star Wars Story, from twenty sixteen, and Solo: A Star Wars Story, from twenty eighteen, are two films already released, focused on the Star Wars universe.

## [08] Sequel  (853 car., ~0.8 min)

The simplest of them, a sequel is nothing more than the continuation of a successful film. It is at these moments that Hollywood usually launches major franchises, as is the case with Fast and Furious, Harry Potter, Paranormal Activity, Scream, Saw and Mission: Impossible. To keep things going, we see the main cast carrying the story forward chronologically, but new characters are also introduced to broaden the plot.

## [09] The Great Train Robbery  (826 car., ~0.8 min)

Remakes, reboots, sequels and the like have been part of the studio machine since at least nineteen oh-four, when the groundbreaking The Great Train Robbery, from nineteen oh-three, was remade and resold. The bad reputation came in the twenty-first century, when studios saw in this a great way to make money more safely and reliably (although it hasn't worked in many cases).

Whenever a new remake, reboot or sequel is announced, there is always uncertainty: which path will the studio, director and screenwriters take? A safe path, as Disney has been doing with its live-action remakes? Or a more auteur-driven path, reimagining old stories in different, contemporary ways, like The Invisible Man, from twenty twenty, Maleficent, from twenty fourteen, Django Unchained, from twenty twelve, and the Planet of the Apes trilogy.
