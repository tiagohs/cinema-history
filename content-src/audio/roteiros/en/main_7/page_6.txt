## [01] Time's Up movement  (667 car., ~0.7 min)

If you follow the film industry, even minimally, you've probably heard of the Time's Up movement or at least heard the term somewhere. Speeches, posters and social media posts, for example, have become opportunities to express the goals of this initiative.

It all began in January twenty eighteen, when a small group of women, all Hollywood celebrities, joined forces to make allegations against Harvey Weinstein (born in nineteen fifty-two), a famous American film producer. The allegations against him are extensive. From rape to sexual harassment, dozens of women came forward to voice their outrage at the producer's conduct, and his career quickly came to an end.

## [02] Learn more: The Harvey Weinstein Case  (869 car., ~0.9 min)

Learn more: The Harvey Weinstein Case.

In twenty seventeen, Harvey Weinstein was accused of sexual harassment by several women, including Angelina Jolie, Gwyneth Paltrow, Mira Sorvino, Rosanna Arquette, Emma de Caunes, Judith Godrèche, Léa Seydoux, Cara Delevingne, Ashley Judd, Rose McGowan, Heather Graham, Brit Marling, Zoe Brock, Lucia Stoller and Louisette Geiss. At least three women accused him of rape: the Italian Asia Argento, the actress Lucia Evans and a third who remains anonymous. The scandal had such an impact that his wife, the English fashion designer Georgina Chapman, filed for divorce from the producer, to whom she had been married for ten years. The case also had professional repercussions: members of the Academy's board voted to expel him from the organization, and he was also fired from the company he himself founded, The Weinstein Company.

## [03] Alyssa Milano  (1274 car., ~1.3 min)

The Time's Up movement was actually inspired by another initiative called #MeToo, which had existed for quite some time and aimed to end sexual violence and raise awareness about the issue. With the accusations against Harvey Weinstein, the movement finally gained the recognition it deserved.

#MeToo focuses on the healing and survival of people who have gone through these abuses, creating a community willing to move forward, however traumatic the experience may have been. Tarana Burke, the movement's founder, says the project is much more than an internet hashtag; it is the beginning of a long conversation for a community seeking healing.

According to the description on the project's own website, #MeToo was created to encourage thousands of people to speak out against sexual harassment and violence, empowering through empathy. Activist Tarana Burke founded #MeToo after a conversation with a girl of just thirteen who told her she had been abused by her mother's boyfriend, in nineteen ninety-six. But it was only on October fifteenth, twenty seventeen that the initiative reached social media, becoming a worldwide sensation after a tweet by actress Alyssa Milano (born in nineteen seventy-two).

## [04] 300 women  (1823 car., ~1.8 min)

Unaware of #MeToo, Alyssa posted on her Twitter: "If you've been sexually harassed or assaulted write 'me too' as a reply to this tweet". At the time, more than sixty-six thousand users replied with the phrase. Due to its success, the actress went on television to talk about the case and, then aware of the project that already existed, took the opportunity to credit the initiative to Burke.

Time's Up, on the other hand, which was inspired by #MeToo, has somewhat different proposals and goals. The initiative seeks a more effective solution to the problem of sexual harassment and abuse, demanding that concrete changes happen, whether regarding safety or equal rights in the workplace. Time's Up was created by a group of more than three hundred women in Hollywood, including actresses such as Natalie Portman (born in nineteen eighty-one) and Reese Witherspoon (born in nineteen seventy-six) and producer and screenwriter Shonda Rhimes (born in nineteen seventy). Christy Haubegger (born in nineteen sixty-eight), an executive at Creative Artists Agency and one of the women who helped found the movement, said that to end sexual harassment it is necessary, first of all, to dissolve inequality, since the lack of balance of power is the root of improper behavior.

"It's a symptom of the larger inequality and pattern of exclusion of women and the lack of balance of power in our businesses. If you want to end sexual harassment, you really need to dissolve those things. So we decided to focus on that", Burke said in an interview with TIME magazine. Thus, both groups now seek changes in policies and legislation with the goal of getting gender parity laws passed, such as equal pay and equal working environments, bringing even more opportunities to women of different races and ethnicities or who are not well paid.

## [05] Learn more: The Kevin Spacey Case (1)  (2145 car., ~2.1 min)

Learn more: The Kevin Spacey Case.

After the scandals involving Harvey Weinstein, victims of sexual abuse in Hollywood have become increasingly vocal when it comes to reporting their abusers. Accused of harassment by several young men, actor Kevin Spacey lost his job, came out publicly as gay and was completely removed from a film for the first time in Hollywood history, all of this in roughly a week.

Anthony Rapp, an actor from Star Trek: Discovery and Rent, revealed that Spacey had tried to coerce him into sexual activity when Rapp was only fourteen. After the case drew wide attention, Spacey wrote an apology on his Twitter, in which he also came out as gay. In light of the accusation, Netflix announced that the next season of House of Cards, the sixth, would be the show's last. A day after the accusation, the International Academy of Television Arts and Sciences declared it would no longer honor Spacey at the International Emmys. In addition, the series This Is Us removed a reference it was going to make to the actor in one of its episodes, and production of the sixth and final season of House of Cards was suspended indefinitely.

Mexican actor Roberto Cavazos gave his testimony via Facebook, stating that Spacey "felt free to touch any man as long as he was under thirty", and that episodes of harassment were common during the time Spacey was artistic director of London's Old Vic theater. The theater opened a confidential complaints process for its staff and actors. In addition to Cavazos's accusation, filmmaker Tony Montana also said, on the same day, that he was harassed by Spacey. "Kevin came up hugging me and right after that he was already insisting that I go home with him. He put his hand on my crotch". An unidentified man claims he woke up with Spacey sleeping on top of him in the mid-eighties, and says he was seventeen at the time. Former bartender Daniel Beal also claims he was harassed by the actor, who exposed himself to him and tried to force him to touch his penis. Spacey bought the bartender's silence with a watch valued at five thousand dollars (approximately fifteen thousand six hundred reais).

## [06] Learn more: The Kevin Spacey Case (2)  (1853 car., ~1.9 min)

Anyone who thinks these cases are old and that the actor changed his behavior is mistaken. Eight members of the crew of House of Cards, the series starring Spacey, also reported having witnessed harassment by the actor. One of the production assistants reported that Spacey put his hand down his pants. "I was in a state of shock. He's a man in a position of a lot of power [on the show] and I was someone very low on the production food chain", says the assistant, who preferred to remain anonymous. MRC, the company that produces the series, already knew of an incident in twenty twelve and sent Spacey to training. After that, no formal complaint was made, but, according to one of the camera operators, crew members never reported the incidents for fear of losing their jobs. "Who's going to believe mere crew members? We'd be fired", he said. Also on November third, a representative for Kevin Spacey stated that the actor was taking time to evaluate himself and seeking treatment, before stating that her company was no longer representing him. On the same day, an actor who preferred not to be identified revealed to The Sun that he woke up to Spacey performing oral sex on him in two thousand eight, while he was working at the Old Vic.

The controversies and accusations involving the actor were so numerous and serious that director Ridley Scott announced he would remove Spacey from his new film, All the Money in the World. This is the first time an actor will be completely removed from a film that has already been shot. The film was already finished, it even had a trailer and an Oscar campaign, and will undergo reshoots, with Christopher Plummer in the role that was previously Spacey's. In addition, actor Jon Bernthal, Netflix's Punisher, worked with Spacey on the set of Baby Driver and said he lost much of the respect he had for him.

## [07] Bryan Singer  (1743 car., ~1.7 min)

The arrival of the Time's Up movement in the media is the reason we now see women taking a stand in awards-show speeches and speaking out against sexual harassment and inequality. With the popularity of the Time's Up movement in the media, especially in the United States, and with the support of Hollywood celebrities, it didn't take long for new allegations to start surfacing. To date, more than ten actors have been accused of harassment and sexual abuse, as well as rape.

[CITAÇÃO] Of course I've dealt with harassment. I don't think there's a single woman in the world who hasn't. I've been doing this for thirty years, and it was very normalized when I started.

said Reese Witherspoon on British radio.

Among them, for example, is Bryan Singer (born in nineteen sixty-five), who has faced accusations since nineteen ninety-seven. The most recent, from twenty seventeen, was made by Cesar Sanchez-Guzman, who sued the director for raping him when he was still a teenager. The accusation led to his being fired from the shoot of Bohemian Rhapsody, from twenty eighteen. Morgan Freeman (born in nineteen thirty-seven) also made the list, accused by a woman who worked on the production of the actor's films. She alleged that Freeman harassed her for several months in twenty fifteen, lifting her skirt and asking whether she was wearing underwear.

While some actors continue to be shunned and remain out of the media, others, unfortunately, end up going on with their lives as usual. Even so, in nearly two years of existence, the movement has managed to show people that these problems exist and need to be fought, whether through punishment or through awareness. That is why the initiative was named Time's Up, which literally means that "time is up".

## [08] Apple TV+  (1027 car., ~1.0 min)

The world is changing and people are less and less tolerant of inequality and injustice, and today it is harder for those who commit these crimes to get away unscathed, even if it takes time. In fact, the trend is for allegations to surface ever more quickly, since no one is as afraid anymore of what might happen.

The importance of the Time's Up and #MeToo movements was, in fact, demonstrated in practice at the close of the decade in the series The Morning Show, which premiered in November alongside the streaming platform Apple TV+. The series tells the story of a morning television program whose pair of hosts are huge celebrities. However, everything changes when Mitch, played by Steve Carell (born in nineteen sixty-two), is accused of abusing coworkers. The series shows what happens behind the scenes after an allegation, and how it affects the production, coworkers and family. The intolerance behind these cases joins with hypocrisy and shows that, from now on, the fight against harassment is only going to grow.
