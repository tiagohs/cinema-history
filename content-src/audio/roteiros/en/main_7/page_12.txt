## [00] Opening  (294 car., ~0.3 min)

Part zero seven: From twenty ten to twenty nineteen. Chapter twelve: Animation Beyond Disney.

From Laika's stop-motion to Makoto Shinkai's anime, from Cartoon Saloon's Irish line work to Sony's Spider-Verse: the decade when animation proved it doesn't belong only to Disney, or only to children.

## [01] Part 1  (800 car., ~0.8 min)

At the box office, the twenty tens belonged to Disney, as we saw in the chapter The Disney Decade. At the Oscars for animation, too: between twenty thirteen and twenty eighteen, the statuette went to Disney or Pixar six years in a row. But one look at the list of nominees tells a different story. In twenty sixteen, for example, four of the five contenders came from outside the House of Mouse: a puppet film for adults, a Brazilian animation drawn with colored pencils, a silent British claymation comedy, and an anime from Studio Ghibli. The decade in which a single studio dominated the market was also the one in which animation became more diverse than ever, with auteurs from Oregon to Kilkenny, from Tokyo to São Paulo, proving that animation is a way of making films, not a children's genre.

## [02] Chris Meledandri  (1908 car., ~1.9 min)

The first counterpoint was commercial. Founded in two thousand seven by Chris Meledandri, a former Fox executive, Illumination partnered with Universal and bet on a cost-controlled formula: Despicable Me, from twenty ten, cost sixty-nine million dollars and grossed five hundred forty-three million dollars worldwide. Its little yellow sidekicks got a film of their own, and Minions, from twenty fifteen, topped one point fifteen billion dollars, the second-highest gross for an animated film up to that point, behind only Frozen. Part of the secret lay in Paris, far from Hollywood's costs: in twenty eleven, the studio bought the animation division of the French company Mac Guff, which had already animated the first film. Illumination proved it was possible to rival Disney at the box office. Aesthetically, however, it was a conservative model: rapid-fire gags, cute characters, zero risk. The revolution would come from elsewhere.

One of them is on the outskirts of Portland, Oregon. Laika, founded in two thousand five and run by Travis Knight, had already drawn attention with Coraline, from two thousand nine, the first stop-motion feature conceived and shot in stereoscopic 3D. Over the following decade, the studio turned frame-by-frame animation, the most handcrafted and slowest technique in filmmaking, into an auteur signature: ParaNorman, from twenty twelve, by Chris Butler and Sam Fell, a zombie story about intolerance; The Boxtrolls, from twenty fourteen; and Kubo and the Two Strings, from twenty sixteen, Knight's directorial debut, a samurai fable inspired by ancient Japan. All five features Laika released between two thousand nine and twenty nineteen were nominated for an Oscar. Kubo won the BAFTA and was only the second animated film in history nominated for the Oscar for visual effects. In twenty twenty, Missing Link, from twenty nineteen, won the Golden Globe, beating Disney.

## [03] Anomalisa  (828 car., ~0.8 min)

If stop-motion could be personal, it could also be adult. Anomalisa, from twenty fifteen, by Charlie Kaufman and Duke Johnson, began as a short film project funded by a Kickstarter campaign that raised four hundred six thousand dollars from five thousand seven hundred seventy backers, twice its goal. It became a feature, and one of the saddest films of the decade: the story of a customer-service speaker in crisis who hears everyone in the world with the same voice, until he meets Lisa. The puppets, made with 3D printing, deliberately show the seam lines on their faces, as if each one's mask were about to fall off. Anomalisa was the first animated film to win the Grand Jury Prize at the Venice Film Festival, in twenty fifteen, and the first R-rated animated film to be nominated for the Oscar for Best Animated Feature.

## [04] Learn more: How Laika makes a film frame by frame  (1125 car., ~1.1 min)

Learn more: How Laika makes a film frame by frame.

In stop-motion, nothing moves on its own. Puppets with articulated metal skeletons are posed by hand, photographed, moved a millimeter and photographed again: up to twenty-four shots for every second of film. The big problem has always been the face. The classic solution is replacement animation, in which the animator swaps the puppet's face for each frame, and Laika took it to another level with 3D printers. Since Coraline, faces have been modeled on the computer and printed in two parts, upper and lower; the seam between them is erased digitally afterward. On Kubo and the Two Strings, color printing even allowed for translucency, and the hero was given more than forty-eight million possible expressions. Production took ninety-four weeks and included a giant skeleton about five meters (sixteen feet) tall, which Laika touted as the largest puppet in stop-motion history. In twenty sixteen, the Academy gave two Laika technicians, Brian McLean and Martin Meunier, a Scientific and Technical Award for pioneering the use of rapid prototyping in character animation.

## [05] Tomm Moore  (1057 car., ~1.1 min)

On the other side of the Atlantic, hand-drawn animation held its ground. In the small town of Kilkenny, Ireland, Cartoon Saloon, founded in nineteen ninety-nine by Tomm Moore, Nora Twomey and Paul Young, turned Celtic folklore into lines, circles and patterns reminiscent of medieval illuminated manuscripts. Moore's Song of the Sea, from twenty fourteen, tells the story of a girl who is a selkie, a creature from Irish legend who lives between sea and land. The Breadwinner, from twenty seventeen, directed by Twomey with Angelina Jolie as executive producer, brought the same handcrafted care to Kabul under the Taliban. Both were nominated for Oscars, and the studio still had Wolfwalkers ahead of it, in twenty twenty. Also in Europe, stop-motion showed it could tackle hard subjects: Ma vie de courgette (My Life as a Zucchini, twenty sixteen), by Swiss director Claude Barras, a Swiss-French coproduction co-written by Céline Sciamma, tells of children in an orphanage through puppets with enormous heads and sad eyes, and was nominated for an Oscar.

## [06] Ernest et Célestine  (876 car., ~0.9 min)

What do these films have in common, besides the Oscars? Often, the same American distributor. New York-based GKIDS, run by Eric Beckman, specialized in bringing foreign and independent animation to U.S. theaters and in running awards campaigns for the Academy. The strategy worked: by twenty nineteen, the company had racked up eleven nominations for Best Animated Feature, a record for an independent distributor in the category, with titles such as Ernest et Célestine (Ernest and Celestine, twenty twelve), Song of the Sea, The Breadwinner and My Life as a Zucchini. Without GKIDS, much of the decade's world animation would have been confined to festivals. And borders grew ever more porous: Studio Ghibli itself coproduced La Tortue rouge (The Red Turtle, twenty sixteen), by Dutch director Michael Dudok de Wit, a dialogue-free film about a castaway, animated in France.

## [07] Alê Abreu  (971 car., ~1.0 min)

It was also through GKIDS in the United States that Brazil made it to the Oscars. Boy and the World, from twenty fourteen, by Alê Abreu, premiered at the Ottawa International Animation Festival in twenty thirteen and opened in Brazilian theaters in January twenty fourteen. The film follows a boy from the countryside who sets out in search of his father and finds factories, slums, advertising and devastation, all seen through a child's eyes. The sets and characters were made with colored pencils, crayons, paint and collage, combined with digital animation, and the sparse dialogue was recorded in backward Portuguese so that the images and music would speak louder: the score brings together Naná Vasconcelos, Barbatuques and a rap by Emicida. It won the Cristal for best feature and the audience award at Annecy, the world's most important animation festival, and was nominated for the twenty sixteen Oscar, the first Brazilian animated feature ever in the running.

## [08] The Wind Rises  (2030 car., ~2.0 min)

In Japan, the decade marked the end of an era. The Wind Rises, from twenty thirteen, a loose biopic of Jiro Horikoshi, the engineer who designed the Zero fighter plane, was announced in twenty thirteen as Hayao Miyazaki's final feature. That same year, Isao Takahata released The Tale of The Princess Kaguya, from twenty thirteen, painted in watercolor and charcoal strokes. After When Marnie Was There, from twenty fourteen, Ghibli announced a pause in feature production, and many thought the studio was finished. It wasn't. Miyazaki, who had "retired" several times before, went back to work: he started storyboarding a new feature in twenty sixteen, and production was made official in twenty seventeen. The result wouldn't arrive until twenty twenty-three: The Boy and the Heron, from twenty twenty-three, which would earn him his second Oscar for Best Animated Feature. But in the twenty tens, the void left by Ghibli opened the door to a new generation.

[CITAÇÃO] My era of making feature-length animated films is absolutely over.

said Hayao Miyazaki, press conference on his retirement, Tokyo, twenty thirteen.

The biggest name of that generation was Makoto Shinkai. With Your Name., from twenty sixteen, the story of two teenagers who swap bodies without ever having met, he made the highest-grossing Japanese film in history worldwide, at about four hundred million dollars, a title it would hold until twenty twenty. In Japan, the film cost four hundred fifty million yen, grossed twenty-five billion yen and spent twelve weekends at number one; at the time, it was the second-highest-grossing Japanese film in the country, behind only Spirited Away. Shinkai would repeat the feat with Weathering with You, from twenty nineteen. Mamoru Hosoda, meanwhile, with Wolf Children, from twenty twelve, and Mirai, from twenty eighteen, made delicate family dramas, and Mirai became the first Japanese animated feature from outside Ghibli to be nominated for an Oscar, in twenty nineteen. Anime was no longer synonymous with a single studio.

## [09] Phil Lord  (913 car., ~0.9 min)

The most visible turning point, however, came from a Hollywood studio. Produced by Phil Lord and Christopher Miller, the duo behind The Lego Movie, and directed by Bob Persichetti, Peter Ramsey and Rodney Rothman, Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse, from twenty eighteen, swapped Peter Parker for Miles Morales, a Black and Puerto Rican teenager from Brooklyn, and told his story as if a comic book had come to life. With a crew of about eight hundred people at Sony Pictures Imageworks, the film grossed more than three hundred fifty million dollars and, on February twenty-fourth, twenty nineteen, broke Disney and Pixar's winning streak at the Oscars. It was the first time since Rango, from twenty eleven, that the statuette had gone to another studio, and Ramsey became the first Black director to win in the category. In the years that followed, many studios would pursue a more graphic, less realistic look.

## [10] Learn more: The Spider-Verse Aesthetic  (1078 car., ~1.1 min)

Learn more: The Spider-Verse Aesthetic.

For more than twenty years, 3D animation chased realism: swaying fur, soft light, a camera that mimics a real lens. Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse went the other way. Most of the film was animated "on twos," meaning each pose stays on screen for two frames, twelve per second, as in traditional hand-drawn animation, giving the movement a crisp, graphic rhythm. Motion blur was abolished. Instead of lens blur, the team knocked the color layers out of register, imitating a badly printed comic book page. Shadows became hatching and halftone dots, sound effects and caption boxes appear in the frame, and a new tool let artists draw ink lines over the 3D characters. Each spider from another dimension has its own style: Spider-Man Noir is black and white, Peni Parker looks straight out of an anime, and Spider-Ham out of a classic Looney Tunes cartoon. Developing the look took more than a year, and with such a painstaking process, the team produced an average of one second of animation per week, compared with the usual four or so.

## [11] Klaus  (2076 car., ~2.1 min)

The decade ended with streaming entering the game. Netflix released Klaus, from twenty nineteen, by Sergio Pablos, a former Disney animator who came up with the original idea for Despicable Me. Pablos set up his own studio, SPA Studios, in Madrid. Hand-drawn, the film used volumetric lighting tools so the characters wouldn't look like stickers on a painted background. It won seven Annie Awards, including Best Feature, as well as the BAFTA for animation. That same year, Netflix acquired distribution rights for almost the entire world to J'ai perdu mon corps (I Lost My Body, twenty nineteen), by Jérémy Clapin: the story of a severed hand that crosses Paris in search of its owner, and the first animated feature to win the Grand Prize of Cannes Critics' Week since the sidebar was created in nineteen sixty-two. At the twenty twenty Oscars, four of the five nominees came from outside Disney, although the statuette went back to Pixar, for Toy Story four, from twenty nineteen.

[CITAÇÃO] When we hear that some kid said "he looks like me," or "they speak Spanish like us," we feel like we already won.

said Phil Lord, Oscar acceptance speech, twenty nineteen.

And then there was China. Ne Zha, from twenty nineteen, by Jiao Zi, is an irreverent retelling of a demon boy from Chinese mythology, inspired by the classic Investiture of the Gods. Released in July, by August it had already overtaken Zootopia as the highest-grossing animated film in the country's history, and it went on to earn more than seven hundred million dollars worldwide, at the time the biggest result ever for an animated film produced outside the United States. It took five years of writing and production and more than one thousand three hundred effects shots made by over twenty Chinese studios. From Oregon stop-motion to São Paulo colored pencils, from Brooklyn comic books to Chinese myth, the twenty tens showed that animation has no owner, no age and no single technique. Disney kept selling more tickets. But many of the ideas that would define animation in the twenty twenties were born far from it.
