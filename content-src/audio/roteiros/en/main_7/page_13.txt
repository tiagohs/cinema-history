## [00] Opening  (286 car., ~0.3 min)

Part zero seven: From twenty ten to twenty nineteen. Chapter thirteen: Foreign Cinema Conquers the Oscars.

From A Separation to Parasite, the decade when Hollywood learned to read subtitles: Iranians, Europeans, Mexico's "Three Amigos" and the Korean wave tear down the one-inch barrier.

## [01] Golden Globe  (861 car., ~0.9 min)

On the night of January fifth, twenty twenty, accepting the Golden Globe for Best Foreign Language Film for Parasite, from twenty nineteen, South Korea's Bong Joon Ho spoke in his own language, with interpreter Sharon Choi at his side, and left behind a line that became a rallying cry: once you overcome the "one-inch-tall barrier of subtitles," you will be introduced to so many more amazing films. The message was aimed at American audiences, notoriously reluctant to read on screen, but it applied just as well to the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. In more than nine decades of Oscars, no film in a language other than English had ever won the top prize. The twenty tens are the story of how that wall was cracked, brick by brick, by filmmakers from Iran, Europe, Mexico and South Korea, until it came crashing down in a single February night.

## [02] Best Foreign Language Film  (1883 car., ~1.9 min)

Cinema from abroad always had a reserved seat at the party, but a seat set apart. Since the nineteen fifty-seven ceremony, when Fellini's La strada, from nineteen fifty-four, inaugurated the competitive race, there has been a Best Foreign Language Film category, in which each country submits a single title chosen by a national committee. Fellini, Bergman and Kurosawa won there, while the main categories remained, with very rare exceptions, reserved for English-language cinema. It was an honorable ghetto: it brought prestige, but rarely opened the way to Best Picture, Director or Screenplay. Anyone who wanted to compete with Hollywood on equal terms generally had to shoot in English. The shift of the twenty tens began precisely when films shot in Persian, German, French, Polish, Hungarian, Spanish and Korean started breaking out of that pen.

[CITAÇÃO] The Oscars are not an international film festival. They're very local.

said Bong Joon Ho, interview with Vulture, twenty nineteen.

The first sign came from Iran. A Separation, from twenty eleven, by Asghar Farhadi, follows a Tehran couple on the verge of divorce who become entangled in a legal case against the family of the woman hired to care for his father, a drama in which no one is entirely innocent. After winning the Golden Bear in Berlin in twenty eleven, the film gave Iran its first Oscar on February twenty-sixth, twenty twelve. Five years later, Farhadi won again with The Salesman, from twenty sixteen, but he didn't go to collect the statuette. In protest against Donald Trump's executive order barring citizens of Iran and six other Muslim-majority countries from entering the United States, he boycotted the February twenty-sixth, twenty seventeen, ceremony. Taking the stage in his place was Iranian American engineer Anousheh Ansari, who read the letter in which the director called the measure an "inhumane law."

## [03] Palme d'Or  (989 car., ~1.0 min)

Europe's auteurs responded with austere, far-from-easy films. Amour, from twenty twelve, by Michael Haneke, about a couple of retired music teachers facing illness and death, won the twenty twelve Palme d'Or and arrived at the Oscars with five nominations, including Best Picture, Director and Actress for Emmanuelle Riva; it won Foreign Language Film for Austria. In twenty fifteen, Poland's Ida, from twenty thirteen, by Paweł Pawlikowski, shot in black and white in the near-square frame of old movie screens, gave the country its first Oscar in the category, with the story of a novice nun who discovers she is Jewish in nineteen sixties Poland. The following year, Hungary's László Nemes made his feature debut with Saul fia (Son of Saul, twenty fifteen): the camera glued to the face of a prisoner forced to work in the crematoria of Auschwitz, with the horror always out of focus in the background. The film won the Grand Prix at Cannes and then the Oscar for Foreign Language Film.

## [04] Alfonso Cuarón  (1024 car., ~1.0 min)

Meanwhile, Mexico was taking over the directing category from within. In twenty fourteen, Alfonso [[pron: Cuarón|Kwa-RONE]] won for Gravity, from twenty thirteen, and became the first Latin American to win Best Director. In twenty fifteen, Alejandro G. [[pron: Iñárritu|Ee-NYAR-ee-too]] took Picture, Director and Screenplay for Birdman or (The Unexpected Virtue of Ignorance), from twenty fourteen; in twenty sixteen, he won Director again for The Revenant, from twenty fifteen, becoming only the third filmmaker to win twice in a row, after John Ford and Joseph L. Mankiewicz. In twenty eighteen, Guillermo del Toro won Picture and Director for The Shape of Water, from twenty seventeen. Together with cinematographer Emmanuel Lubezki, the record holder with three consecutive Oscars, the "Three Amigos," as the press dubbed [[pron: Cuarón|Kwa-RONE]], [[pron: Iñárritu|Ee-NYAR-ee-too]] and del Toro, changed the face of the awards. There was, however, one catch: almost all of this was in English. Hollywood had learned to absorb foreign talent. What remained was to accept foreign films in their own language.

## [05] Golden Lion  (927 car., ~0.9 min)

That step came with Roma, from twenty eighteen. [[pron: Cuarón|Kwa-RONE]] returned to the Mexico City of his childhood, in the early nineteen seventies, to tell the life of Cleo, an Indigenous domestic worker played by newcomer Yalitza Aparicio. The film, shot in black and white and spoken in Spanish and Mixtec, was financed and distributed by Netflix (the chapter on streaming tells that part of the story). After the company pulled its films from Cannes over a rule requiring a release in French theaters, Roma won the Golden Lion in Venice. At the Oscars, it received ten nominations, tying the record for a non-English-language film, and Yalitza became the first Indigenous Mexican woman nominated for Best Actress. On February twenty-fourth, twenty nineteen, it took home three awards: Director, Cinematography (by [[pron: Cuarón|Kwa-RONE]] himself) and Foreign Language Film, Mexico's first. The top prize, however, went to Green Book, from twenty eighteen.

## [06] Zimna wojna  (913 car., ~0.9 min)

The twenty nineteen race was perhaps the strongest in the category's history. Alongside Roma was Pawlikowski's Zimna wojna (Cold War, twenty eighteen), a black-and-white romance that crosses a divided Europe, inspired by the story of the director's parents; the film won him the directing prize at Cannes and three Oscar nominations, including Best Director. Also competing were Shoplifters, from twenty eighteen, by Hirokazu Kore-eda, winner of the twenty eighteen Palme d'Or, about a clan of small-time Tokyo thieves that forces viewers to ask what, in the end, makes a family; Lebanon's Capernaum, from twenty eighteen; and Germany's Werk ohne Autor (Never Look Away, twenty eighteen), by Florian Henckel von Donnersmarck. The fact that two of the five Best Director nominees were competing with films in Spanish and Polish showed that voters no longer drew such a rigid line between "our" cinema and "theirs."

## [07] Best International Feature Film  (822 car., ~0.8 min)

The Academy itself had been changing. After #OscarsSoWhite (the subject of the chapter on diversity), it announced in January twenty sixteen a goal of doubling the number of women and members of color by twenty twenty, and began inviting ever larger and more international classes: in twenty nineteen alone, eight hundred forty-two invitations went out to professionals from fifty-nine countries, half of them women. On April twenty-third, twenty nineteen, came the symbolic change. The category was renamed Best International Feature Film because, according to committee co-chairs Larry Karaszewski and Diane Weyermann, the word "foreign" had become outdated within the global filmmaking community. The shortlist also grew, from nine to ten films. The first ceremony under the new name would be in February twenty twenty.

## [08] Learn more: From "Foreign Language" to "International"  (1127 car., ~1.1 min)

Before the category existed, the Academy honored films from abroad with honorary statuettes: the first went to the Italian Sciuscià (Shoeshine, nineteen forty-six), by Vittorio De Sica, in the late nineteen forties, and eight films were recognized this way through nineteen fifty-five. With competitive voting, starting in nineteen fifty-seven, came the rule that has never changed: each country except the United States submits a single film, chosen by a local committee, and the dialogue must be predominantly in a language other than English. Until the mid-two thousands, the film also had to be in an official language of the submitting country. Italy is the biggest winner, with fourteen awards counting the honorary ones. The twenty nineteen name change didn't resolve every contradiction: months later, in November, the Academy disqualified Nigeria's Lionheart, from twenty eighteen, by Genevieve Nnaji, for having too much dialogue in English, which happens to be Nigeria's official language. "International," after all, was still defined by distance from English.

## [09] BAFTA  (930 car., ~0.9 min)

No country, however, came as prepared as South Korea. In twenty sixteen, Park Chan-wook brought The Handmaiden, from twenty sixteen, to Cannes, transposing a Victorian novel by Sarah Waters to nineteen thirties Korea under Japanese occupation. The country chose to submit a different title to the Oscars, but the film won the BAFTA for Best Film Not in the English Language in twenty eighteen. That same year, Lee Chang-dong adapted Haruki Murakami in Burning, from twenty eighteen, which won the international critics' prize at Cannes; in December twenty eighteen, it became the first Korean film to make the Academy's shortlist. Bong had already tested the West with English-language productions such as Snowpiercer, from twenty thirteen, and Okja, from twenty seventeen, but it was by going back to shooting in Korean that he went furthest. Until then, South Korea had never received a single Oscar nomination, in any category.

## [10] Screen Actors Guild  (843 car., ~0.8 min)

On May twenty-fifth, twenty nineteen, Parasite won the Palme d'Or, Korea's first. Ironically, the jury president was [[pron: Iñárritu|Ee-NYAR-ee-too]], who described the decision as "absolutely unanimous." In the United States, the film was released by independent distributor Neon, and the campaign became a phenomenon: Bong's interviews with interpreter Sharon Choi circulated on social media, the Kim family became a meme and, in January twenty twenty, the cast won the ensemble award from the Screen Actors Guild (SAG), a first for a non-English-language film. Then came six Oscar nominations, the first in the country's history: Picture, Director, Original Screenplay, Film Editing, Production Design and International Feature. The Academy now had before it a film that American audiences were watching, discussing and understanding, even while reading subtitles.

## [11] Learn more: Inside Parasite  (1018 car., ~1.0 min)

Learn more: Inside Parasite.

The Park mansion doesn't exist. It was built from scratch as a set by production designer Lee Ha-jun, and Bong designed it as a world unto itself, full of corners where characters could spy on one another or hide from their employers. The houses of the rich and the poor were built on open-air lots, chosen with an eye to the position of the sun: the poorer the home, the less light it gets. Bong himself called Parasite a "stairway movie," and Lee admitted he had never built so many staircases for a film. The acknowledged reference is Kurosawa's High and Low, from nineteen sixty-three, in which the rich man lives at the top of the hill and the kidnapper watches him from below. Parasite has more layers: the Kim family climbs up to the Parks' living room, discovers that there are people living even further down, in the secret basement, and, on the night of the storm, descends step after step to their flooded semi-basement. The geography of the house is the social hierarchy itself.

## [12] Han Jin-won  (728 car., ~0.7 min)

On the night of February ninth, twenty twenty, Parasite won four Oscars. First, Original Screenplay, for Bong and Han Jin-won; then International Feature, the first awarded under the new name; then Director. In his speech, Bong recalled a line that had stayed with him since his student days and pointed to its author in the audience, Martin Scorsese, who received a standing ovation. Finally, Best Picture. For the first time in ninety-two ceremonies, the top prize went to a film not in English and, for the first time since Marty, from nineteen fifty-five, the same film took both the Palme d'Or and the Academy's highest honor. The "one-inch barrier" had not disappeared for audiences, but inside the Academy, it had fallen.

## [13] Anna Muylaert  (2031 car., ~2.0 min)

Brazil watched all of this from the audience. Anna Muylaert's The Second Mother, from twenty fifteen, earned Regina Casé and Camila Márdila an acting award at Sundance and was the country's Oscar submission for twenty sixteen, but it didn't make the final list. The team behind Aquarius, from twenty sixteen, by Kleber Mendonça Filho and starring Sonia Braga, protested at Cannes against the impeachment proceedings against President Dilma Rousseff; months later, the selection of Little Secret, from twenty sixteen, as the Oscar submission stirred controversy, with filmmakers questioning the impartiality of the committee that made it. In twenty nineteen, Kleber and Juliano Dornelles won the Jury Prize at Cannes with Bacurau, from twenty nineteen; Invisible Life, from twenty nineteen, by Karim Aïnouz, won the Un Certain Regard section and represented Brazil at the Oscars, again without a nomination. Nominations came through other routes: Boy and the World, from twenty fourteen, in animation, and The Edge of Democracy, from twenty nineteen, by Petra Costa, in documentary. In the foreign-language category, there had been no Brazilian nominee since Central Station, from nineteen ninety-eight.

[CITAÇÃO] The most personal is the most creative.

said Bong Joon Ho, quoting Martin Scorsese, Oscar acceptance speech, twenty twenty.

When the lights went down at the Dolby Theatre in February twenty twenty, the decade ended with a clear tally. In ten years, Iranian, Austrian, Polish, Hungarian, Mexican, Japanese and Korean filmmakers had pushed the Oscars out of their comfort zone, helped by a more international Academy, by European festivals that served as showcases and by streaming money. None of this erased the inequality in distribution or much of the audience's resistance to subtitles. But Bong's message had gotten through to the voters: a film could come from anywhere and speak any language. The door was open, and the years that followed, told in the chapter The World Beyond Hollywood, would show who would walk through it.
