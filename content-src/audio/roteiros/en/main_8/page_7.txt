## [01] Streaming wars  (778 car., ~0.8 min)

On November twelfth, twenty nineteen, Disney launched Disney+ and signed up more than ten million subscribers in a single day. It was the starting gun for a battle the American press soon dubbed the streaming wars. If the previous decade had belonged to the rise of Netflix, told in the chapter "The streaming revolution that changed Hollywood," the twenty twenties began with every major studio convinced it needed a Netflix of its own. Each would stop licensing films and shows to the competition and bet on its own library to attract subscribers. What looked like a race for audiences turned into a war of attrition, too expensive for almost every contender. It would end in mergers, layoffs, shelved films and, six years later, the sale of one of Hollywood's oldest studios.

## [02] Hamilton  (1704 car., ~1.7 min)

The race moved fast. HBO Max launched on May twenty-seventh, twenty twenty; NBCUniversal's Peacock followed on July fifteenth; and the old CBS All Access became Paramount+ on March fourth, twenty twenty-one. The pandemic, which shut theaters and scrambled release windows (the subject of the chapter "The Screens Go Dark"), acted as an accelerant. Disney premiered the musical Hamilton directly on its platform, Warner released Dune, from twenty twenty-one, in theaters and on HBO Max on the same day, and the service even bankrolled Zack Snyder's Justice League, from twenty twenty-one, Zack Snyder's version of a film that had flopped in twenty seventeen, born of a fan campaign on social media. By March twenty twenty-one, Disney+ had already passed one hundred million subscribers. Wall Street rewarded a single number, subscriptions, and the companies spent whatever it took to make it grow.

Prestige followed. On March twenty-seventh, twenty twenty-two, Sian Heder's CODA, from twenty twenty-one, acquired by Apple TV+ at the Sundance Film Festival, became the first film from a streaming service to win the Oscar for Best Picture, a trophy Netflix had been chasing for years. Ten days earlier, on March seventeenth, Amazon had closed its eight point forty-five billion dollars purchase of MGM, the studio of the roaring lion, founded in nineteen twenty-four. For the first time, an online retailer owned a classic Hollywood studio, complete with a library of thousands of titles and the James Bond franchise. In February twenty twenty-five, Amazon went further and took creative control of double-oh-seven, which the Broccoli family of production company Eon had guarded since the nineteen sixties.

## [03] Warner Bros. Discovery  (864 car., ~0.9 min)

On the other side, AT&T, which had bought Time Warner in twenty eighteen, gave up on Hollywood. On April eighth, twenty twenty-two, its WarnerMedia division merged with Discovery, owner of documentary and reality TV channels, giving birth to Warner Bros. Discovery, led by David Zaslav. The new company started life with tens of billions of dollars in debt, and Zaslav promised three billion dollars in savings. The cuts showed up on screen: series and films vanished from HBO Max overnight, such as the sci-fi drama Westworld, pulled in late twenty twenty-two, and projects were canceled. In August twenty twenty-two, the company even shelved a virtually finished film, Batgirl. In twenty twenty-three, HBO Max was merged with Discovery+ and rebranded simply as Max; in twenty twenty-five, in a bid to win back the brand's prestige, it went back to being HBO Max.

## [04] Learn more: The Batgirl affair  (1288 car., ~1.3 min)

Learn more: The Batgirl affair.

On August second, twenty twenty-two, Warner Bros. Discovery announced that Batgirl would be released neither in theaters nor on streaming. Directed by Adil El Arbi and Bilall Fallah, the film had cost about ninety million dollars and was virtually finished. Leslie Grace played Barbara Gordon, J.K. Simmons was Commissioner Gordon, Brendan Fraser played the villain Firefly, and Michael Keaton returned as Batman. Commissioned by the previous management as an HBO Max exclusive, it no longer fit the new strategy, and the company preferred to write off the loss for tax purposes rather than spend more to release it. The animated Scoob! Holiday Haunt met the same fate. In a statement, the studio said the decision did not reflect Leslie Grace's performance and thanked the directors and casts of both films, but the case became a symbol of an era in which a finished film can simply disappear. In twenty twenty-three, history repeated itself with Coyote versus Acme, from twenty twenty-six, a comedy starring Wile E. Coyote of Looney Tunes. After protests from artists and fans, the film was sold in twenty twenty-five to the small Ketchup Entertainment and reached U.S. theaters in late August twenty twenty-six, opening in second place at the box office.

## [05] Part 4  (996 car., ~1.0 min)

The most symbolic shock, however, came from Netflix itself. On April nineteenth, twenty twenty-two, the company announced it had lost two hundred thousand subscribers in the first quarter, its first decline in more than a decade, and its stock plunged. The pioneer was admitting that the market had limits and that more than one hundred million households were watching the service on someone else's password. Its response changed the entire business. On November third, twenty twenty-two, it launched a basic plan with ads, priced at six point ninety-nine dollars a month in the United States. On May twenty-third, twenty twenty-three, it began charging for password sharing outside the home: seven point ninety-nine dollars a month per "extra member" in the U.S. Viewers grumbled, but they paid. By the end of twenty twenty-four, Netflix had passed three hundred million subscriptions, and starting in twenty twenty-five it stopped reporting the number, as if to signal that that race was over.

## [06] Growth at all costs  (826 car., ~0.8 min)

The episode marked the end of the era of growth at all costs. From then on, Wall Street wanted to see profits, and every service followed the same playbook: higher prices, ad-supported tiers, fewer releases and a wave of cancellations. At Disney, losses in the streaming division reached one point forty-seven billion dollars in a single quarter of twenty twenty-two, and the board fired CEO Bob Chapek. On November twentieth of that year, Bob Iger, who had stepped down in twenty twenty, returned to the helm. The company then bought out Comcast's stake in Hulu, in a negotiation that cost about nine billion dollars between twenty twenty-three and twenty twenty-five, and began folding Hulu into Disney+ in a single app. Bundles, ads, packaged channels: streaming, born to topple cable TV, was starting to look just like it.

## [07] David Ellison  (1073 car., ~1.1 min)

The same logic pushed Paramount. Heir to a studio with roots going back to nineteen twelve, the Redstone family's company was too small for the war and, in twenty twenty-four, agreed to be bought by Skydance, the production company of David Ellison, son of billionaire Larry Ellison, founder of Oracle. David Ellison had produced films in the Mission: Impossible series, such as Mission: Impossible - The Final Reckoning, from twenty twenty-five, and Top Gun: Maverick, from twenty twenty-two, the hit that waited for theaters to reopen before it premiered. The eight billion dollars deal needed U.S. government approval, and politics entered the picture. In July twenty twenty-five, Paramount paid sixteen million dollars to settle a lawsuit Donald Trump had brought against CBS's sixty Minutes. Shortly afterward, CBS announced the end of the late-night talk show hosted by Stephen Colbert, a critic of the president, citing financial reasons. The regulatory agency FCC approved the merger on July twenty-fourth, and the deal closed on August seventh, twenty twenty-five.

## [08] Learn more: Warner Bros.: 100 years of a studio up for sale  (1072 car., ~1.1 min)

Learn more: Warner Bros.: one hundred years of a studio up for sale.

In April twenty twenty-three, Warner Bros. turned one hundred. The studio founded by brothers Harry, Albert, Sam and Jack Warner was the one that bet on sound and released The Jazz Singer, from nineteen twenty-seven, the first feature-length talkie. It made the gangster pictures of the nineteen thirties, Casablanca, from nineteen forty-two, the Looney Tunes cartoons and, later, the films of Stanley Kubrick, the Harry Potter saga and Christopher Nolan's Batman. Starting in nineteen ninety, it passed from hand to hand within ever-larger conglomerates: Time Warner, AT&T and, since twenty twenty-two, Warner Bros. Discovery. The centennial was celebrated amid Zaslav's cuts. Less than three years later, the studio was up for sale, fought over by a company that had started out renting DVDs by mail and by another that had just taken over Paramount, Warner's rival since the silent era. In October twenty twenty-six, the famous water tower bearing the WB shield in Burbank got the name of a new owner.

## [09] Netflix  (872 car., ~0.9 min)

Then came the endgame. In June twenty twenty-five, Warner Bros. Discovery announced it would split in two: on one side, the studios, HBO, HBO Max and DC; on the other, the TV networks, such as CNN and Discovery, in a company called Discovery Global. Ellison's Paramount began making offers for the entire company in late September and saw its bids rejected, until, on October twenty-first, Warner acknowledged it had received proposals and opened an auction. On December fifth, twenty twenty-five, came the surprise: the winner was Netflix, which would pay twenty-seven point seventy-five dollars a share for the studios, HBO and HBO Max, in a seventy-two billion dollars deal (eighty-two point seven billion dollars including debt). Three days later, Paramount launched a hostile bid: thirty dollars a share, in cash, for the whole company, made directly to shareholders.

## [10] James Cameron  (2161 car., ~2.2 min)

The idea of the platform that had most threatened movie theaters buying a century-old studio provoked a rare reaction in Hollywood. The directors' guild and the exhibitors' association, Cinema United, said they feared for the future of moviegoing, and James Cameron wrote to a senator that the deal would be "disastrous" for theatrical cinema. There was also the antitrust problem: combined, Netflix and HBO Max would hold an enormous share of subscription streaming, and Trump himself said it "could be a problem." At a Senate hearing in February twenty twenty-six, Ted Sarandos, Netflix's co-CEO, pledged to keep a forty-five-day theatrical window for Warner films and argued that about eighty percent of HBO Max subscribers already subscribed to Netflix. Critics of the rival bid, for their part, pointed to the Ellisons' close ties to the Trump administration.

In the end, money settled it. Paramount raised its bid to thirty-one dollars a share, and on February twenty-sixth, twenty twenty-six, Warner's board deemed it "superior." Netflix chose not to match, saying the deal was "no longer financially attractive," and walked away with a two point eight billion dollars breakup fee, paid by Paramount. The acquisition, worth about eighty-one billion dollars (nearly one hundred eleven billion dollars including debt), was approved by shareholders in April. The irony was that the studio on the auction block was enjoying its best stretch in years. After a string of twenty twenty-five hits, including A Minecraft Movie, from twenty twenty-five, Superman, from twenty twenty-five, and Weapons, from twenty twenty-five, Warner took home eleven Oscars on March fifteenth, twenty twenty-six, tying the record for the most wins by a single studio in one ceremony. Paul Thomas Anderson's One Battle After Another, from twenty twenty-five, won Best Picture, and Ryan Coogler's Sinners, from twenty twenty-five, took home four statuettes after setting a record with sixteen nominations.

## [11] Skydance  (1888 car., ~1.9 min)

Resistance remained. In July twenty twenty-six, the attorneys general of twelve states, led by California's Rob Bonta, went to court to block the merger, arguing that it would wipe out competition and harm, among others, moviegoers. The Writers Guild filed suit as well, and thousands of Hollywood professionals signed an open letter against the deal. The settlement with the states, approved by a judge on September thirtieth, required the new company to release at least thirty films a year in U.S. theaters for the first two years and thirty-two a year for the following three, to freeze what it charges exhibitors for three years, and to create an editorial independence board for CBS and CNN. On October sixth, twenty twenty-six, the acquisition closed. The company was renamed Skydance, with David Ellison as CEO and Ynon Kreiz, former chief executive of Mattel and an executive producer of Barbie, from twenty twenty-three, as co-CEO.

[CITAÇÃO] Today is a historic day, not just for Skydance, but for our entire industry.

said David Ellison, at the closing of the Warner Bros. Discovery acquisition, twenty twenty-six.

What does this mean for cinema? Of the Big Five of Hollywood's golden age, MGM, Paramount, Warner, Fox and RKO, none is its own master anymore. RKO stopped producing in the nineteen fifties, and the other four now belong to three groups: Amazon; Disney, which bought Fox in twenty nineteen; and Skydance. Consolidation has practical consequences: fewer buyers for writers' and directors' projects, fewer doors open to mid-budget films, and film libraries that hinge on a corporation's accounting, as the Batgirl case showed. For theaters, the promise of thirty films a year is a guarantee that never needed to be put in writing before, and it lasts only five years. The streaming wars produced no clear winner. They produced survivors, and there are fewer of them every year.
