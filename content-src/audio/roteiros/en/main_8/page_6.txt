Part zero eight: From twenty twenty to today. Chapter six: The World Beyond Hollywood.

After Parasite, films in languages other than English take over the Oscars and the box office, from Japan to India, from China to Korea. Until Brazil, at last, wins its first Academy Award with I'm Still Here.

## [01] Parasite  (811 car., ~0.8 min)

When Parasite, from twenty nineteen, won the Oscar for Best Picture on February ninth, twenty twenty, many treated the victory as a happy accident, the kind of exception that only proves the rule. There was reason for doubt: it had taken ninety-two ceremonies for a non-English-language film to win the top prize, and nothing guaranteed that the next one wouldn't put everything back to normal. A month later, the pandemic shut down movie theaters around the world, and the question hung in the air: would the door opened by Bong Joon Ho stay open? The answer came gradually, from different places and not always via the Academy's stage, but it came. In the years that followed, films from Korea, Japan, India, Europe and China showed that Parasite had not been a fluke. And at the end of the road stood Brazil.

## [02] Minari  (1839 car., ~1.8 min)

The following year brought a curious case. Minari, from twenty twenty-one, directed by Lee Isaac Chung, is an American production, made by the son of Korean immigrants and spoken mostly in Korean. As a result, the Golden Globes placed it in the foreign-language film category, out of the running for Best Picture, a widely criticized decision. At the Oscars on April twenty-fifth, twenty twenty-one, Youn Yuh-jung, playing the grandmother who comes from Korea to live with the family in Arkansas, won Best Supporting Actress and became the first Korean actress honored by the Academy. In twenty twenty-two, it was Japan's turn. Drive My Car, from twenty twenty-one, directed by Ryusuke Hamaguchi, a three-hour drama based on a Haruki Murakami short story (the opening credits don't appear until about forty minutes in), received four nominations, including Best Picture, a first for a Japanese film, and won the Oscar for International Feature.

On March twelfth, twenty twenty-three, the night belonged to Everything Everywhere All at Once, from twenty twenty-two. An American A24 production directed by the duo Daniel Kwan and Daniel Scheinert and starring a predominantly Asian cast, the multiverse comedy about a Chinese immigrant who owns a laundromat won seven Oscars, including Best Picture. Michelle Yeoh became the first Asian woman to win Best Actress, and Ke Huy Quan, the kid from Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom who had spent nearly two decades away from the camera, took Supporting Actor. At the same ceremony, Germany's Im Westen nichts Neues (All Quiet on the Western Front, twenty twenty-two), directed by Edward Berger, won four statuettes (International Feature, Cinematography, Original Score and Production Design) and showed that Netflix had gotten the message: a war film in German could also be a global product.

## [03] RRR  (913 car., ~0.9 min)

India reached the stage through a side door. The country did not pick RRR, from twenty twenty-two, as its official submission (the slot went to the Gujarati-language drama Chhello Show), so director S. S. Rajamouli and his team mounted an independent campaign in the United States. It paid off: the Telugu-language epic about two revolutionaries who take on British rule had already become a phenomenon on Netflix, where it spent weeks among the most-watched films. The musical number "Naatu Naatu" won the Golden Globe and then the Oscar for Original Song, the first ever for a song from an Indian film. RRR also symbolizes a shift within India itself: released dubbed into several languages, it is a landmark of pan-Indian cinema, in which productions from the country's south, and no longer just Bollywood, dominate the national box office. In twenty twenty-four, the top earner was Pushpa two, also in Telugu.

## [04] Godzilla Minus One  (911 car., ~0.9 min)

On March tenth, twenty twenty-four, Japan left the ceremony with two awards that said a lot about the new landscape. Godzilla Minus One, from twenty twenty-three, directed by Takashi Yamazaki, cost less than fifteen million dollars, according to the director, a fraction of the budget of an American blockbuster, and still won the Oscar for Visual Effects over Hollywood productions. It was the franchise's first Oscar nomination in nearly seventy years, and the win made Yamazaki, who also supervised the effects, the first director to win in the category since Stanley Kubrick for two thousand one: A Space Odyssey. That same night, The Boy and the Heron, from twenty twenty-three, gave Hayao Miyazaki his second Oscar for Animated Feature, more than twenty years after Spirited Away. A cheap monster and an eighty-three-year-old master proved that Japanese cinema didn't need to imitate Hollywood to beat it.

## [05] Anatomie d'une chute  (935 car., ~0.9 min)

The same twenty twenty-four ceremony had two European films among the ten Best Picture nominees. Anatomie d'une chute (Anatomy of a Fall, twenty twenty-three), directed by Justine Triet and winner of the Palme d'Or at Cannes, wasn't even chosen by France to compete for International Feature (the country preferred The Taste of Things), but it made it into the main categories on its own and won Original Screenplay. The Zone of Interest, from twenty twenty-three, directed by Jonathan Glazer, a British production spoken almost entirely in German, won International Feature and Sound with the story of the family of the Auschwitz commandant, who live next to the death camp as if nothing were happening. Germany's Sandra Hüller was in the lead cast of both. Four years after Parasite, non-English-language films were no longer exotic visitors in the top race: they were real contenders, judged by an Academy that had changed its face.

## [06] Emilia Pérez  (962 car., ~1.0 min)

That openness also brought a new kind of scrutiny, and the case of Emilia Pérez, from twenty twenty-four, shows it. French director Jacques Audiard's musical, spoken almost entirely in Spanish and set in Mexico but shot on a soundstage near Paris, arrived at the twenty twenty-five Oscars with thirteen nominations, a record for a non-English-language film. The campaign quickly fell apart. In Mexico, critics and relatives of the disappeared accused the film of treating drug trafficking and its victims with stereotypes and superficiality, and of lacking Mexican leads. Days after the nominations, old racist and Islamophobic posts by Karla Sofía Gascón, the first openly trans actress nominated for an Oscar, resurfaced, and Netflix sidelined her from the campaign. The film walked away with two awards: Supporting Actress, for Zoe Saldaña, and Original Song, for "El Mal." The lesson was clear: at a more global Oscars, it also matters who tells whose story.

## [07] Ne Zha 2  (948 car., ~0.9 min)

Far from the Academy's party, the shift showed up at the box office. Released on January twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-five, the first day of the Chinese New Year, Ne Zha two, from twenty twenty-five, directed by Jiaozi, grossed about two point twenty-seven billion dollars, nearly all of it in China, becoming the highest-grossing animated film in history and the biggest hit ever for a non-English-language film. More than four thousand professionals from one hundred thirty-eight Chinese studios worked on it. Months later, Korean culture conquered the planet by another route: KPop Demon Hunters, from twenty twenty-five, an American production from Sony Pictures Animation directed by Maggie Kang and Chris Appelhans, about a K-pop group that hunts demons, premiered on Netflix in June and became the most-watched film in the platform's history. In March twenty twenty-six, it won the Oscars for Animated Feature and Original Song, for "Golden."

## [08] I'm Still Here  (1072 car., ~1.1 min)

And then it was Brazil's turn. I'm Still Here, from twenty twenty-four, directed by Walter Salles, adapts Marcelo Rubens Paiva's memoir about his mother, Eunice, who watched her husband, former congressman Rubens Paiva, be taken from their home by agents of Brazil's military dictatorship in January nineteen seventy-one, never to return. Awarded for its screenplay at the Venice Film Festival, the film opened in Brazilian theaters on November seventh, twenty twenty-four, and drew more than five million moviegoers, the largest audience for a Brazilian film since the pandemic. On January fifth, twenty twenty-five, Fernanda Torres won the Golden Globe for Best Actress in a Drama, beating Nicole Kidman, Angelina Jolie and Kate Winslet, and became the first Brazilian to win in an acting category. There was something of a reckoning in it: more than twenty-five years earlier, her mother, Fernanda Montenegro, who appears in the film as the elderly Eunice, had been nominated for an Oscar for Central Station, from nineteen ninety-eight, also directed by Walter Salles.

Marcelo Rubens Paiva's book, published in twenty fifteen, was written when his mother, Eunice, was already living with Alzheimer's, so it is also about memory slipping away. After her husband's disappearance, Eunice was jailed and interrogated for twelve days, raised their five children on her own, earned a law degree and became a leading advocate for Indigenous rights. Only in nineteen ninety-six, after a long battle she led, did the Brazilian state issue Rubens Paiva's death certificate. Walter Salles's film turned this family story into a national conversation, in the year the nineteen sixty-four military coup marked its sixtieth anniversary. Calls for a boycott from the far right did not stop audiences from packing theaters. Reality kept pace with fiction: on January twenty-third, twenty twenty-five, the same day the film received its three Oscar nominations, the certificate was amended to record a death that was "unnatural, violent, caused by the Brazilian state."

## [10] The Secret Agent  (2158 car., ~2.2 min)

The Oscar ceremony, on March second, twenty twenty-five, fell on a Carnival Sunday, and Brazil watched as if it were a World Cup final. The film was competing in three categories, including Best Picture, the first nomination for an entirely Brazilian production in the top race. Fernanda Torres lost Best Actress to Mikey Madison of Anora, but when Penélope Cruz announced I'm Still Here as Best International Feature, the celebration spilled into the streets. At the Sambadrome in Rio and on the sound trucks of Salvador's Carnival, where singer Daniela Mercury shouted "dictatorship never again" into the microphone, revelers celebrated with replica statuettes and Fernanda Torres costumes. It was the first Oscar ever for a Brazilian production. Until then, the closest the country had come was Black Orpheus, which won in nineteen sixty with a Brazilian cast but as France's entry.

The success was no isolated lightning strike. In May twenty twenty-five, The Secret Agent, from twenty twenty-five, directed by Kleber Mendonça Filho, a thriller set in Recife during the nineteen seventy-seven Carnival, at the height of the dictatorship, won the Best Director and Best Actor awards at Cannes, the latter for Wagner Moura, the first Brazilian to win Best Actor at the festival. Kleber, for his part, became the second Brazilian honored for directing at Cannes, after Glauber Rocha in nineteen sixty-nine. In January twenty twenty-six, the film won two Golden Globes (Non-English Language Film and Actor in a Drama) and soon after received four Oscar nominations: Best Picture, Actor, International Feature and Casting. Moura was the first Brazilian ever nominated for Best Actor. At the March fifteenth, twenty twenty-six, ceremony, however, the film went home empty-handed, beaten by Norway's Sentimental Value, by Michael B. Jordan for Sinners and by One Battle After Another. Even so, Brazil had reached the Best Picture race for the second year in a row.

## [11] Part 10  (867 car., ~0.9 min)

What changed between Parasite and The Secret Agent? Partly, who votes: the Academy became more international and began to see with fresh eyes films that once reached American members only with a great deal of luck. Partly, how people watch: streaming made subtitles a habit, and titles like RRR and All Quiet on the Western Front found American audiences through Netflix. And, most important, what gets told: deeply local films, about a Korean family in Arkansas, two Indian revolutionaries against the British Empire or a Brazilian mother searching for her disappeared husband, proved they didn't need to water themselves down to speak to the world. Hollywood remains cinema's biggest stage, but it is no longer the only one. For Brazil, which celebrated an Oscar the way it celebrates a goal, the lesson was that its own memory, told with courage, can be universal.

Part of the explanation for the shift lies in the voter rolls. After #OscarsSoWhite in twenty sixteen, the Academy began inviting hundreds of new members each year, with far more women, people of color and professionals from outside the United States. The share of international members rose from thirteen percent in twenty sixteen to about twenty percent in twenty nineteen, and the Academy, which had roughly six thousand members in twenty sixteen, tops eleven thousand with the class of twenty twenty-six. In the class of twenty twenty-five, fifty-five percent of the five hundred thirty-four invitees came from outside the U.S., among them ten Brazilians, such as Fernanda Torres, director Daniela Thomas and the screenwriters of I'm Still Here, Murilo Hauser and Heitor Lorega. Wagner Moura had already been invited in twenty twenty-one. In the class of twenty twenty-six, fifty-three percent of the five hundred twenty-nine invitees were from abroad. When Drive My Car was nominated for Best Picture, the American press linked the feat to this renewal. More voters from Seoul, Paris, Mumbai and São Paulo mean more people who watch a subtitled film without treating it as "foreign," and campaigns that have to speak to the whole world.
