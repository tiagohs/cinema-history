## [00] Opening  (289 car., ~0.3 min)

Part zero eight: From twenty twenty to today. Chapter three: The New Wave of Horror.

After the pandemic, horror became the safest bet at the box office. From Smile and Barbarian to The Substance and Sinners, the genre broke out of its niche, won over the Oscars and spread around the world.

## [01] The Invisible Man  (949 car., ~0.9 min)

The twenty tens ended with horror rehabilitated, and the twenty twenties began with it in the lead. When The Invisible Man, from twenty twenty, hit theaters in late February twenty twenty, no one imagined that, weeks later, cinemas around the world would close their doors because of the pandemic. When they reopened, the genre showed an advantage blockbusters lacked: cheap films with loyal audiences that depend neither on expensive stars nor on effects that eat up hundreds of millions of dollars. In twenty twenty-one, according to the website The Numbers, horror accounted for twelve point nine percent of tickets sold in the United States, the highest share ever recorded for the genre up to that point (surpassed only in twenty twenty-five). What looked like a side effect of an empty market became the rule: in the years that followed, horror established itself as Hollywood's most reliable bet and achieved a prestige it had rarely enjoyed.

## [02] Smile  (903 car., ~0.9 min)

The pandemic also taught studios to send horror straight to streaming, and it was test screenings that changed their minds. Smile, from twenty twenty-two, Parker Finn's feature debut, had been made for Paramount+; after very positive test screenings, Paramount took it to theaters in September twenty twenty-two. It cost seventeen million dollars and earned two hundred seventeen million dollars, helped by a simple and unsettling marketing stunt: actors smiling fixedly behind home plate during televised baseball games. Around the same time, Barbarian, from twenty twenty-two, escaped being sent straight to Hulu. Written and directed by Zach Cregger, a comedian from the troupe The Whitest Kids U' Know, the film begins as a thriller about a double-booked rental house and changes course several times. Made on a four point five million dollars budget, it grossed forty-six million dollars worldwide.

## [03] Blumhouse  (905 car., ~0.9 min)

If Blumhouse was horror's assembly line, A24 became its designer label. The distributor of The Witch and Hereditary, from twenty eighteen, used the pandemic for an experiment: Ti West shot two features back to back, in secret, in New Zealand, with the same star, Mia Goth. In X, from twenty twenty-two, a tribute to nineteen seventies slashers about a crew shooting an adult film on a Texas farm, Goth plays both young Maxine and the elderly Pearl. Months later, Pearl, from twenty twenty-two, told the origin story of the murderous old woman in the saturated colors of old musicals, and the trilogy wrapped up with MaXXXine, from twenty twenty-four. In twenty twenty-three, the distributor went to YouTube to find the Australian brothers Danny and Michael Philippou, whose Talk to Me, from twenty twenty-three, became A24's highest-grossing horror film up to that point, a story told in the next chapter.

## [04] Learn more: A24: the brand that became a genre  (1408 car., ~1.4 min)

Learn more: A24: the brand that became a genre.

Founded in New York in August twenty twelve by Daniel Katz, David Fenkel and John Hodges, A24 did something rare in the film business: it turned a distributor's logo into a promise of style. For many viewers, "an A24 movie" became almost a subgenre, with a slow pace, an oppressive atmosphere, a static camera and endings that don't explain everything. The company cultivated that identity with auteur-style posters, merchandise for fans and messaging that treats every release as a cinephile event. Its credibility also came from awards: in twenty twenty-three, Everything Everywhere All at Once, from twenty twenty-two, gave the distributor its second Best Picture Oscar, after Moonlight in twenty seventeen. In horror, that prestige helped popularize the label elevated horror, used by the American press to set "auteur" films apart from "ordinary" horror. Many genre critics reject the term: in twenty eighteen, Jacob Knight wrote in SlashFilm that the distinction ignores classics like The Exorcist and The Texas Chain Saw Massacre, which were already art and entertainment at the same time. The twenty twenties proved him right: in twenty twenty-six, the highest-grossing film in A24's history was Backrooms, from twenty twenty-six, an adaptation of a YouTube web series that passed four hundred million dollars worldwide, a story told in another chapter.

## [05] Neon  (876 car., ~0.9 min)

A24's direct competitor on this turf is Neon, the distributor that made its name with Palme d'Or winners such as Parasite and Titane, from twenty twenty-one. Its biggest ace in the genre was Longlegs, from twenty twenty-four, by Osgood Perkins, son of Anthony Perkins, the Norman Bates of Psycho. The release campaign became a case study: teasers just a few seconds long, coded messages, an encrypted ad in the Seattle Times that evoked the Zodiac Killer, billboards with a phone number that played sinister recordings, and not a single clear image of the villain played by Nicolas Cage. The mystery worked as bait. In July twenty twenty-four, the film opened with twenty-two point four million dollars in the United States, the biggest opening in Neon's history, and finished with one hundred twenty-eight million dollars worldwide, having cost less than ten million dollars.

## [06] The Substance  (1909 car., ~1.9 min)

No film sums up the new wave better than The Substance, from twenty twenty-four, by French director Coralie Fargeat. In the story, a fading TV star, fired on her fiftieth birthday, injects a substance that generates a younger version of herself, on one condition: the two must switch places every seven days. The rest is an escalation of blood, prosthetics and satire about Hollywood's obsession with youth. Awarded for its screenplay at Cannes in twenty twenty-four, the film was dropped by Universal in a dispute over final cut, ended up being distributed by MUBI and became the company's biggest box-office hit. It earned five Oscar nominations, including Best Picture, Director and Actress, and won for Makeup and Hairstyling. For Demi Moore, it was a career turnaround: a Golden Globe, a Screen Actors Guild Award and the first Oscar nomination of her life.

Prestige horror also looked back. Robert Eggers, who had made his debut with The Witch, realized a project he had been chasing since his teens: remaking Nosferatu, eine Symphonie des Grauens (Nosferatu, nineteen twenty-two), F. W. Murnau's expressionist classic. His Nosferatu, from twenty twenty-four, traded the vampire's cut-out silhouette for a decomposing count, nearly unrecognizable under Bill Skarsgård's makeup, and placed at its center Lily-Rose Depp's Ellen, a young woman tormented since childhood by a presence that desires her. Released in the United States on Christmas twenty twenty-four, it cost fifty million dollars and grossed one hundred eighty-two million dollars, the biggest box office of Eggers's career. It received four Oscar nominations, for Cinematography, Costume Design, Production Design and Makeup, but won none: it lost the Makeup award, of all things, to The Substance.

## [07] Jason Blum  (1008 car., ~1.0 min)

At the other end, popular horror had its own boom. Jason Blum's Blumhouse finalized its merger with James Wan's Atomic Monster in January twenty twenty-four, and had its biggest hit with a film critics loathed: Five Nights at Freddy's, from twenty twenty-three, an adaptation of an indie video game. Even though it was released simultaneously on the Peacock streaming service, it grossed two hundred ninety-seven million dollars worldwide, Blumhouse's highest-grossing film up to that point, propelled by fans who had grown up watching the game on YouTube. Damien Leone, meanwhile, financed part of Terrifier two, from twenty twenty-two, through online crowdfunding: the film cost two hundred fifty thousand dollars, earned fifteen point eight million dollars and became notorious for reports of viewers getting sick during screenings. In October twenty twenty-four, Terrifier three opened at No. one in the United States and, with ninety million dollars, became the highest-grossing unrated film in history.

## [08] Learn more: The body as horror: the body horror revival  (1275 car., ~1.3 min)

Learn more: The body as horror: the body horror revival.

Body horror, the horror born of the body's own transformations, mutilations and revolts, has a patron saint: David Cronenberg, who turned the genre into a study of desire, technology and disease in films like The Fly, from nineteen eighty-six, winner of the Oscar for Makeup. In the twenty twenties, this kind of cinema left the midnight screenings and moved to the center of the festivals. In twenty twenty-one, Julia Ducournau won the Palme d'Or for Titane, the story of a killer who becomes pregnant after an encounter with a car; she was only the second woman to win the prize, after Jane Campion in nineteen ninety-three. The following year, Cronenberg himself returned to Cannes with Crimes of the Future, from twenty twenty-two. The Substance carried the trend to the Oscars and, in twenty twenty-six, the Makeup category also nominated the Norwegian Den stygge stesøsteren (The Ugly Stepsister, twenty twenty-five), a gory retelling of Cinderella told from the side of the stepsister who mutilates her own body to fit the slipper. The thread connecting these films is clear: the body became the stage on which the anxieties of the era play out, from aging and beauty standards to motherhood and gender identity.

## [09] Jordan Peele  (787 car., ~0.8 min)

Between these two poles, Jordan Peele kept making films that defy pigeonholing. After Get Out and Us, he released Nope, from twenty twenty-two, a blend of western, science fiction and monster movie about a brother and sister who train horses for Hollywood productions and try to photograph something hiding in the clouds above the family ranch. Shot partly with IMAX cameras, the film is also a reflection on spectacle: on who profits from the image and who gets devoured by it. It grossed one hundred seventy-one million dollars worldwide. It did not have the impact of Get Out, but it reinforced what Peele had shown in the previous decade: horror could be the space for Black filmmakers to tell the American story on their own terms. Three years later, that idea would reach its peak.

## [10] Sinners  (2162 car., ~2.2 min)

That peak goes by the name of Sinners, from twenty twenty-five. Written and directed by Ryan Coogler, the film is set in nineteen thirty-two Mississippi: twins Smoke and Stack, both played by Michael B. Jordan, return to their hometown to open a blues joint and, on opening night, are visited by vampires. Coogler uses the myth to talk about cultural heritage, the appropriation of Black music and the violence of the segregated South. With a budget of ninety million dollars to one hundred million dollars, the film grossed three hundred seventy million dollars worldwide, an exceedingly rare result for an original story. At the twenty twenty-six Oscars, it received sixteen nominations, an all-time record that surpassed the fourteen earned by All About Eve, Titanic and La La Land. It won four: Actor for Jordan, Original Screenplay for Coogler, Original Score for Ludwig Göransson and Cinematography for Autumn Durald Arkapaw, the first woman to win in that category. Best Picture went to One Battle After Another.

Sinners did not come alone: twenty twenty-five was Warner Bros.'s year in the genre. The Conjuring: Last Rites, from twenty twenty-five, closed out the Warrens' saga with four hundred ninety-nine million dollars, the highest gross in the series, and Zach Cregger, of Barbarian, returned with Weapons, from twenty twenty-five. Its premise is a fairy-tale mystery: at two:seventeen one morning, seventeen children from the same class run out of their homes and vanish into the dark, and only one student is left behind. Told in chapters, each from a different character's point of view, the film mixes horror, acid humor and a tension many read as an allegory for the collective traumas of American towns. It cost thirty-eight million dollars and reached two hundred seventy million dollars worldwide. The scene-stealer was veteran Amy Madigan as Aunt Gladys: forty years after her only previous nomination, she won the Oscar for Best Supporting Actress in March twenty twenty-six.

## [11] Exhuma  (946 car., ~0.9 min)

While Hollywood was discovering the genre's prestige, the rest of the world was proving its commercial muscle. In South Korea, Exhuma, from twenty twenty-four, by Jang Jae-hyun, follows a shaman, her apprentice, a mortician and a geomancer hired to move a cursed grave and, beneath the scares, probes the wounds of the Japanese occupation. Released in February twenty twenty-four, it drew eleven point nine million moviegoers and was the country's first occult film to pass ten million admissions. In Indonesia, the phenomenon is even bigger: in twenty twenty-four, about sixty percent of the two hundred fifty-eight local films released were horror, and the genre sold fifty-four point six million tickets. The most respected name in this wave is Joko Anwar, whose Pengabdi Setan two: Communion (Satan's Slaves two: Communion, twenty twenty-two) was the first Indonesian film released in IMAX and drew six point three million people to theaters.

## [12] Cuando acecha la maldad  (1122 car., ~1.1 min)

Some of the decade's most frightening films came from Latin America. Argentina's Cuando acecha la maldad (When Evil Lurks, twenty twenty-three), by Demián Rugna, about a demonic possession spreading through the countryside, became in twenty twenty-three the first Latin American film to win Best Picture at the Sitges Film Festival. In Mexico, Michelle Garza Cervera made her debut with Huesera: The Bone Woman, from twenty twenty-three, a body horror film about a mother-to-be haunted by a figure of twisted bones, which won awards at Tribeca. And Guillermo del Toro finally made his Frankenstein, from twenty twenty-five, which took home three Oscars in twenty twenty-six; added to the four for Sinners and Amy Madigan's, that made eight statuettes for monster stories in a single night. From Australia, besides Talk to Me, came Late Night with the Devil, from twenty twenty-four, a fake nineteen seventy-seven talk show that ends in tragedy. Six years after theaters went dark, horror no longer asks permission: it packs theaters, wins awards and keeps turning each era's fears into a collective experience in the dark.
