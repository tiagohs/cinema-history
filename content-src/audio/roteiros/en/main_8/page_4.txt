Part zero eight: From twenty twenty to today. Chapter four: Internet Creators Hit the Big Screen.

From the Philippou brothers to Kane Parsons, Curry Barker and Markiplier, filmmakers who came up on YouTube traded their channels for film sets and, in twenty twenty-six, dominated the horror box office.

## [01] Part 1  (857 car., ~0.9 min)

For nearly a century, the road to the director's chair had few doors: film school, a short screened at festivals, commercials, music videos, television. In the twenty twenties, another one opened, used by a generation that learned to shoot, edit and scare in front of millions of viewers on YouTube. On the opening weekend of Backrooms, at the turn of May into June twenty twenty-six, the British trade magazine Screen Daily summed up the phenomenon in a headline: YouTube creators were "dominating" the U.S. box office. That season, two of the most-watched films in the United States were horror movies directed by twentysomething first-timers who had started out on their own channels. The road there, however, took nearly two decades and ran through filmmakers who used the internet first as a showcase and only later as studio, distributor and audience.

## [02] Fede Álvarez  (1838 car., ~1.8 min)

The first signs came from short films that went viral. In two thousand nine, Uruguayan filmmaker Fede Álvarez posted ¡Ataque de Pánico! on YouTube, a roughly three hundred dollars short in which giant robots attack Montevideo. The video landed him a deal with Sam Raimi's production company and, four years later, the director's chair on Evil Dead, from twenty thirteen. Sweden's David F. Sandberg followed a similar path with Lights Out, a twenty thirteen short starring his wife, Lotta Losten, that spread across Reddit: the feature version, Lights Out, from twenty sixteen, produced by James Wan, cost five million dollars and grossed one hundred forty-nine million dollars. In both cases, the internet served as a calling card. The video opened Hollywood's door, but the online audience didn't come along, and the films were marketed like any other studio horror movie.

The artist who proved that someone born on the internet could make auteur cinema about the internet itself was Bo Burnham. He started posting comic songs on YouTube in two thousand six, while still a teenager, became a stand-up comedian and made his directorial debut with Eighth Grade, from twenty eighteen, released by A24: the story of a thirteen-year-old girl who records advice videos that almost nobody watches. The film earned him the Directors Guild of America award for outstanding first-time feature director and the Writers Guild of America award for best original screenplay. On May thirtieth, twenty twenty-one, in the middle of the pandemic, came Inside, a Netflix special that Burnham wrote, shot, lit and edited by himself, inside a single room. The special won three Emmys, including for directing and writing, as well as a Grammy. Burnham became a touchstone for the next generation: someone who turned the language of the bedroom screen into art.

## [03] Danny Philippou  (1092 car., ~1.1 min)

The breakthrough came from Australia. In twenty thirteen, in Adelaide, brothers Danny Philippou and Michael Philippou launched the channel RackaRacka, famous for violent parodies packed with homemade effects, such as a psychopathic Ronald McDonald and a battle between Harry Potter and the characters of Star Wars; the channel won a Streamy for best international channel. Alongside the channel, the two learned the craft on a professional set, as crew members on The Babadook, from twenty fourteen. The duo's first feature, Talk to Me, from twenty twenty-three, premiered at the Adelaide Film Festival in October twenty twenty-two, was bought by A24 at Sundance and hit theaters in July twenty twenty-three. Made for four point five million dollars, it grossed ninety-two million dollars worldwide, became A24's highest-grossing horror film to date and won eight Australian Academy awards, including best film. Their second feature, Bring Her Back, from twenty twenty-five, starring Sally Hawkins, scored eighty-nine percent on Rotten Tomatoes and proved the brothers were no one-hit wonder.

## [04] Kyle Edward Ball  (877 car., ~0.9 min)

Meanwhile, in Canada, Kyle Edward Ball ran the channel Bitesized Nightmares, where he re-created nightmares sent in by viewers. With about fifteen thousand dollars, much of it crowdfunded, he shot Skinamarink, from twenty twenty-three, in a week, in the house where he grew up in Edmonton: just over an hour and a half of dark hallways, toys on the floor and the glow of a CRT television, about two children who wake up in the middle of the night to find that the doors and windows have vanished. After its premiere at the Fantasia Festival in July twenty twenty-two, a technical glitch let the film leak, and clips spread across TikTok. In January twenty twenty-three, the feature opened in U.S. theaters, where it made about two million dollars. Audiences were split down the middle, but the message stuck: the aesthetic born in online horror videos worked in a dark theater.

## [05] Kane Parsons  (1018 car., ~1.0 min)

The short that took the Backrooms from online forums to the big screen is the work of Kane Parsons, born in two thousand five in Petaluma, California, the son of a visual effects artist. He was sixteen when he posted on his channel Kane Pixels, on January seventh, twenty twenty-two, the tape of a nineteen nineties cameraman who falls into the yellow labyrinth and is chased by a creature. The video grew into a series, and in February twenty twenty-three A24 announced the feature, with Parsons himself, then seventeen, directing. Backrooms, from twenty twenty-six, opened on May twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-six, with Chiwetel Ejiofor and Renate Reinsve in the cast and a budget under ten million dollars. It debuted with eighty-one point five million dollars in the United States, the biggest opening in A24's history, and made Parsons, at twenty, the youngest filmmaker ever to top the North American box office. It went on to pass four hundred million dollars worldwide, with an eighty-six percent critics' score.

## [06] Curry Barker  (1160 car., ~1.2 min)

The season's other phenomenon came from comedy. Curry Barker, born in nineteen ninety-nine in Alabama, dropped out of film school in Los Angeles to make sketches for YouTube and TikTok with his friend Cooper Tomlinson as the duo that's a bad idea. In twenty twenty-three, his horror short The Chair topped ten million views and caught producers' attention; the following year, he released Milk and Serial, from twenty twenty-four, for free on YouTube, a found footage feature that cost eight hundred dollars. Obsession, from twenty twenty-six, his first film made for theaters, cost at most seven hundred fifty thousand dollars, according to Barker, and was bought by Focus Features for about fifteen million dollars after the twenty twenty-five Toronto International Film Festival. Released in the U.S. on May fifteenth, twenty twenty-six, it grew in its second weekend, something extremely rare for a horror film in wide release, passed five hundred million dollars worldwide and became the year's highest-grossing horror film and the biggest in Focus's history. Barker has already been tapped to direct a new version of The Texas Chain Saw Massacre for A24.

## [07] Learn more: Analog horror and liminal spaces: the horror born on YouTube  (1306 car., ~1.3 min)

On May twelfth, twenty nineteen, an anonymous user on 4chan's paranormal board asked for disturbing images that "felt off" and opened the thread with a photo of an empty room with carpet, yellow wallpaper and fluorescent lighting (taken in two thousand two in a former furniture store in Oshkosh, Wisconsin). Another user replied with a text about an infinite maze of identical rooms: the Backrooms. The image helped popularize the aesthetic of liminal spaces, everyday places emptied of their function and strangely familiar. At the same time, analog horror was growing on YouTube, imitating VHS tapes, emergency broadcasts and nineteen eighties and nineteen nineties TV, with degraded images and almost no cheap jump scares. The name came from Local fifty-eight, a series by Kris Straub launched in twenty fifteen; it was followed by Gemini Home Entertainment, from twenty nineteen, and The Mandela Catalogue, from twenty twenty-one. An heir to the found footage of The Blair Witch Project, from nineteen ninety-nine, this kind of horror bets on what the image hides. In January twenty twenty-two, a teenager brought the two currents together in a VHS-style short animated in Blender; the video went on to top ninety million views.

## [08] Mark Fischbach  (1159 car., ~1.2 min)

If Parsons and Barker reached the movies with help from studios, Mark Fischbach, aka Markiplier, wanted to do it all himself. Since twenty twelve, he has amassed more than thirty-eight million subscribers playing indie horror games like Five Nights at Freddy's, whose film adaptation, Five Nights at Freddy's, from twenty twenty-three, made two hundred ninety-seven million dollars in twenty twenty-three with YouTubers in cameo roles and just a thirty-three percent approval rating from critics. Markiplier's own film, Iron Lung, from twenty twenty-six, an adaptation of David Szymanski's twenty twenty-two game, was written, directed, edited, starred in and financed by Markiplier, on a budget that, according to him, came to between four million dollars and five million dollars. The plan was to show the film in sixty independent theaters. Fans began asking their local theaters to book it, and chains such as AMC, Regal and Cinemark signed on. On January thirtieth, twenty twenty-six, Iron Lung opened in more than three thousand theaters with eighteen point two million dollars, and went on to pass fifty million dollars worldwide despite mixed reviews.

## [09] Chris Stuckmann  (2013 car., ~2.0 min)

The most ironic case is that of Chris Stuckmann. A YouTube film critic since two thousand nine with more than two million subscribers, he announced in twenty twenty-one that he would stop trashing bad movies (he no longer wanted to "tear down" filmmakers) and focus on discussing how films are made. His first feature, Shelby Oaks, from twenty twenty-five, partly funded by fans on Kickstarter, follows a woman searching for her missing sister, a ghost-hunting YouTuber, and starts out as found footage before turning into a conventional narrative. After its premiere at the Fantasia Festival in twenty twenty-four, it gained Mike Flanagan as executive producer and distribution from Neon, which paid for extra days of shooting. Released in October twenty twenty-five, it made about eight million dollars worldwide, earned a fifty-five percent approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and a C+ audience grade from CinemaScore. The critic who used to judge others became a target of criticism himself and discovered that loyal followers are not enough to fill theaters.

[CITAÇÃO] I think this could be a huge win for independent film.

said Markiplier, on a YouTube livestream after the premiere of Iron Lung, twenty twenty-six.

What changes when a director arrives with an audience already in tow? For the studios, the risk seems smaller: the film is born with built-in marketing and an idea already tested in the comments. After Backrooms and Obsession, executives began scouring YouTube, TikTok and Instagram for new directors. Neon hired Sam Evenson to turn his short Mora into a feature, and Lionsgate and Blumhouse brought in Dylan Clark, a maker of YouTube horror shorts, to direct a new Blair Witch film. Skepticism, however, persists. An agent quoted by the newsletter The Ankler called the rush "embarrassing" and noted that Hollywood is "still run by eighty-one-year-olds who don't watch YouTube." Journalist Ryan Broderick doubted that any YouTuber, even one as big as MrBeast, could single-handedly draw crowds to a film.

## [10] Porta dos Fundos: Contrato Vitalício  (1139 car., ~1.1 min)

In Brazil, the jump from the internet to the big screen took a different route. Back in the twenty tens, the comedy group Porta dos Fundos, founded in twenty twelve, brought its humor to theaters with Porta dos Fundos: Contrato Vitalício, from twenty sixteen, which sold about four hundred sixty thousand tickets. That same year, It's a Fairy!, from twenty sixteen, starring YouTuber Kéfera Buchmann, drew one point seven million moviegoers but was savaged by critics. In twenty seventeen, Whindersson Nunes, who then ran the country's biggest channel, shared top billing with Tom Cavalcante in Os Parças, from twenty seventeen, and Internet - The Movie, from twenty seventeen, packed several YouTubers into a single cast. In Brazil, creators came in as the stars of comedies made by the traditional industry, not as the authors of their own films. The closest thing to the American model is Luccas Neto, who, after Os Aventureiros: A Origem, from twenty twenty-three, made with Warner, used his own company to finance Príncipe Lu e a Lenda do Dragão, from twenty twenty-four, featuring a special appearance by comedy legend Renato Aragão.

## [11] Learn more: From channel to set: how to fund a film with your own audience  (1263 car., ~1.3 min)

Learn more: From channel to set: how to fund a film with your own audience.

For creators with an audience of their own, the public can also be the investor, the distributor and the marketing team. In twenty twenty-two, Chris Stuckmann raised one million three hundred ninety thousand eight hundred forty-five dollars from fourteen thousand seven hundred twenty backers on Kickstarter to shoot Shelby Oaks, the best-funded horror campaign in the platform's history. Markiplier went further: he paid for Iron Lung out of his own pocket, negotiated directly with theaters and said that, with no major studio in the middle, he was splitting the box office with exhibitors "basically fifty/fifty." He then registered with YouTube as a film distributor (a so-called aggregator), which let him release the feature on the platform itself in May twenty twenty-six, with no middlemen; the DVDs and Blu-rays he printed at home. Curry Barker didn't ask fans for money, but he used his free videos as a portfolio: his feature Milk and Serial, released for free, earned him a contract with a major agency. In each case, the channel plays a different role: funding source, distributor or showcase. The model's limit is clear: an audience guarantees an opening, not word of mouth.

## [12] Sidney Lumet  (980 car., ~1.0 min)

Film history has seen this movie before. In the nineteen fifties, directors trained in live television, such as Sidney Lumet, arrived in Hollywood; in the nineteen nineties, it was the turn of those who came from music videos, like David Fincher and Spike Jonze. Internet creators bring something similar: a visual language shaped in front of small screens, with handheld cameras, degraded images, empty spaces and the dread of what lies outside the frame. And they bring something new: an audience that has followed them for years, tests every idea in the comments and feels a sense of ownership over the project. The risk, as skeptics point out, is that the industry will mistake subscriber counts for talent. The bet is that, among thousands of channels, lie some of the great auteurs of the next decade.

[CITAÇÃO] By the time they get to the movie, they've already had a billion test screenings.

said Mike De Luca, co-chairman of Warner Bros. Pictures Group, to the AP, twenty twenty-six.
