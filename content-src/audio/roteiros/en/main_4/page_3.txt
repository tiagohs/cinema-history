## [00] Opening  (213 car., ~0.2 min)

Part zero four: From nineteen sixty to nineteen sixty-nine. Chapter three: New Hollywood.

A new era arrived in Hollywood in the nineteen sixties, with new directors who would go on to become landmarks of the craft.

## [01] James Bond  (1613 car., ~1.6 min)

In the nineteen fifties, the old studio system and even the idea that Hollywood was the world center of cinema were toppled. In the following decade, Hollywood - like all of American society - lived through a state of upheaval.

The court-ordered separation of the major studios' production and distribution arms was followed by a proliferation of "runaway productions" - big American productions shot abroad to use box office receipts frozen in countries whose laws required money earned there to be spent there, or to take advantage of resources such as cheaper labor. Even so, the major studios managed to stay afloat as Los Angeles replaced New York as the main center of television production.

Judging by the box office and awards lists, one might think little had changed. Big musicals and historical epics dominated the nineteen sixties, although newer genres such as the technically British James Bond series and spaghetti Westerns made their way into the landscape.

The double-oh-seven films were born of the same climate of paranoia responsible for American productions such as The Manchurian Candidate, from nineteen sixty-two. Spaghetti Westerns turned Clint Eastwood (born in nineteen thirty) of Rawhide, from nineteen fifty-one, into a solid Hollywood icon, but their production was one hundred percent Italian. Hollywood stayed in the game, even if James Coburn's agent Derek Flint was no match for Sean Connery's unbeatable James Bond. Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid, from nineteen sixty-nine, and The Wild Bunch, from nineteen sixty-nine, put a new face on the Italian reinvention of the Western.

## [02] Cleopatra  (1327 car., ~1.3 min)

It was also a time of crisis for the old way of doing things. The excesses of Cleopatra, from nineteen sixty-three, brought Twentieth Century Fox to the brink of bankruptcy and the era of epic cinema to an end. Similarly, films such as Doctor Dolittle, from nineteen sixty-seven, showed that big musicals - in many ways the greatest success story of the nineteen sixties - were not infallible.

The idea that a studio should pour most of its resources into one or two blockbusters took several blows during the decade. The veteran executives at Universal and Columbia who saw profits sprouting from films born of the nineteen sixties counterculture, such as Easy Rider, from nineteen sixty-nine, without understanding their appeal, were dethroned in the New Hollywood by younger producers such as Robert Evans, of Paramount.

Still, some points would yet be scored by film industry veterans such as Billy Wilder (nineteen oh-six to two thousand two). His comedy-drama The Apartment, from nineteen sixty, starred his favorite, Jack Lemmon (nineteen twenty-five to two thousand one), as a lonely insurance clerk devoted to currying favor with senior managers by lending them his apartment for their extramarital affairs.

## [03] Some Like It Hot  (1113 car., ~1.1 min)

Following in the footsteps of the immensely popular Some Like It Hot, from nineteen fifty-nine, The Apartment was a huge commercial success that won five Oscars, including Best Director and Best Picture, even though its treatment of adultery drew disapproval in some quarters.

The new young directors - some coming from television - such as Arthur Penn (nineteen twenty-two to twenty ten), Norman Jewison (born in nineteen twenty-six), Mike Nichols (born in nineteen thirty-one), and Sydney Pollack (nineteen thirty-four to two thousand eight), were open to the influence of the new Europe and of old Hollywood. Blake Edwards (born in nineteen twenty-two) had begun to make his name with his comedies, especially the popular Pink Panther series. He directed Audrey Hepburn (nineteen twenty-nine to nineteen ninety-three) in one of her most important roles, the quirky call girl Holly Golightly in Breakfast at Tiffany's, from nineteen sixty-one. Sidestepping the seedier aspects of Truman Capote's original, Edwards used his flair for the genre to make it one of the most memorable romantic comedies of the decade.

## [04] Cary Grant  (1194 car., ~1.2 min)

In the early nineteen sixties, the lists of movie stars were dominated by performers such as Cary Grant, Elizabeth Taylor, Frank Sinatra, and John Wayne. By the end of the decade, the leading names in the film pantheon were Paul Newman, Warren Beatty, Faye Dunaway, Robert Redford, Dustin Hoffman, Sidney Poitier, Jane Fonda, and Barbra Streisand. And it was not just a matter of youth and beauty: Charles Bronson and Lee Marvin, former supporting tough guys, were elevated to leading roles in action films, while the star of the highest-grossing American film of nineteen sixty-six, Who's Afraid of Virginia Woolf?, from nineteen sixty-six, was Elizabeth Taylor (nineteen thirty-two to twenty eleven), an old-guard actress.

Adapted from Edward Albee's play, the film, with only four characters, shows Taylor spewing obscenities and Richard Burton (nineteen twenty-five to nineteen eighty-four) taking all the punishment. The choice of the famous beauty to play the frumpy Martha raised doubts, but her vigorous performance alongside stars such as Burton, George Segal (born in nineteen thirty-four), and Sandy Dennis (nineteen thirty-seven to nineteen ninety-two) ended up being highly praised.

## [05] John Cassavetes  (1498 car., ~1.5 min)

In New York, John Cassavetes (nineteen twenty-nine to nineteen eighty-nine) - who kept up a film acting career to support his work as a writer - was experimenting freely with experimental cinema, while Andy Warhol (nineteen twenty-eight to nineteen eighty-seven) turned his cameras on drug addicts, prostitutes, losers, and drag queens to turn them into "superstars". American directors such as Stanley Kubrick (nineteen twenty-eight to nineteen ninety-nine) and Richard Lester (born in nineteen thirty-two) preferred to work in Britain, which had experienced its own New Wave. One of Kubrick's great films of the decade was the black comedy Dr. Strangelove or: How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love the Bomb, from nineteen sixty-four.

In the United States, newcomer filmmakers such as John Schlesinger (nineteen twenty-six to two thousand three), John Boorman (born in nineteen thirty-three), and Roman Polanski (born in nineteen thirty-three) cast a fresh eye on the country in vigorous, unsettling films. The elegant revenge thriller Point Blank, from nineteen sixty-seven, directed by John Boorman (born in nineteen thirty-three) and adapted from a pulp fiction classic, starred Lee Marvin (nineteen twenty-four to nineteen eighty-seven), who had asked for the Englishman to take the director's chair.

## [06] Midnight Cowboy  (760 car., ~0.8 min)

Marvin plays a lone killer who takes on a large, impersonal corporation hidden behind a cloak of respectability. The film's striking scenes and frenetic pace show the influence of the French New Wave. Point Blank proved more interesting to directors than to contemporary audiences, although its reputation has grown steadily ever since.

One of the most powerful films of the late decade, Midnight Cowboy, from nineteen sixty-nine, directed by fellow foreigner Schlesinger, shed light on America's underclass and the taboo of male prostitution. The drama boosted the career of then-unknown actor Jon Voight (born in nineteen thirty-eight), popularized Harry Nilsson's melancholy theme song, and won three Academy Awards, including Best Picture and Best Director.

## [07] Russ Meyer  (1185 car., ~1.2 min)

The history of nineteen sixties Hollywood films can also be viewed through the lens of changing censorship standards and sociopolitical attitudes. It was a time of transition between the Rat Pack and Woodstock, between John Kennedy's idealistic Camelot and the fervent mobilizations for civil rights and against the Vietnam War.

The old nineteen ninety Motion Picture Production Code was on its way out, and the new rating system had to take into account the more overt treatment of sex, violence, and profanity. By the end of the decade, "mainstream" films were trying to show and say things that had until then been the exclusive domain of exploitation-film specialists such as Russ Meyer (nineteen twenty-two to two thousand four).

At the end of the anything-goes nineteen sixties, Meyer was invited to make a "big picture" at Twentieth Century Fox - Beyond the Valley of the Dolls, from nineteen seventy. The final shootouts of Bonnie and Clyde, from nineteen sixty-seven, by Arthur Penn (nineteen twenty-two to twenty ten), and The Wild Bunch, from nineteen sixty-nine, by Sam Peckinpah (nineteen twenty-five to nineteen eighty-four), are marked by unprecedented levels of violence.

## [08] Rock Hudson  (1807 car., ~1.8 min)

The suggestive comedies of Rock Hudson (nineteen twenty-five to nineteen eighty-five) and Doris Day (nineteen twenty-two to twenty nineteen), which tread carefully on sexual themes, gave way to neurotic, cynical essays on modern mores, very much in the spirit of the times, such as The Graduate, from nineteen sixty-seven. Liberal attitudes were piling up: Spencer Tracy (nineteen hundred to nineteen sixty) was forced to accept Sidney Poitier (born in nineteen twenty-seven) as a prospective son-in-law in Guess Who's Coming to Dinner, from nineteen sixty-seven, although Black audiences preferred him demanding respect from Rod Steiger in In the Heat of the Night, from nineteen sixty-seven: Norman Jewison's (born in nineteen twenty-six) film, whose protagonist is an African American detective investigating a murder in a racist small town in Mississippi, reflects the tension of the civil rights era.

[CITAÇÃO] This used to be a helluva good country. I can't understand what's gone wrong with it

said George Hanson (Jack Nicholson) in Easy Rider.

Thanks to his work in late masterpieces by John Ford (eighteen ninety-four to nineteen seventy-three) and Howard Hawks (eighteen ninety-six to nineteen seventy-seven), John Wayne remained a box office champion, finally winning an Oscar for True Grit, from nineteen sixty-nine. Clint Eastwood was the cowboy hero of younger audiences, who even liked their police heroes - such as Steve McQueen (nineteen thirty to nineteen eighty) in Bullitt, from nineteen sixty-eight, - to be alienated, impassive, and anti-establishment. Directed by another English expatriate, Peter Yates (born in nineteen twenty-nine), Bullitt is still famous for its long car chase through the streets of San Francisco, unprecedented in film history, which earned editor Frank P. Keller an Oscar.

## [09] A Man for All Seasons  (1473 car., ~1.5 min)

Even in the safest genres, traditionally Oscar winners, the signs of the times could be felt: in the historical film A Man for All Seasons, from nineteen sixty-six, an anti-authoritarian rebel is crushed by the system, much like the bikers gunned down by truckers in Easy Rider.

Let us not forget that Oliver!, from nineteen sixty-eight, is a tuneful musical about crooks, murderers, pedophiles, and prostitutes comparable to Joe Buck and Ratso Rizzo in Midnight Cowboy, from nineteen sixty-nine. The decade's first Best Picture Oscar winner, The Apartment, from nineteen sixty, deals with disillusionment with the American dream; the last, Midnight Cowboy, is about the lives of outcasts and the marginalized who cannot even aspire to the desperate position of Billy Wilder's corporate parasite.

Times would keep changing. Producer Roger Corman (born in nineteen twenty-six) would soon establish a policy of giving experience and opportunities to young talent, which would allow emerging innovators such as Francis Ford Coppola (born in nineteen thirty-nine) and Martin Scorsese (born in nineteen forty-two) to write and direct their own films. In nineteen sixty-nine, barely out of his teens, Steven Spielberg (born in nineteen forty-six) directed Joan Crawford (nineteen oh-four to nineteen seventy-seven) in a segment of the pilot for the TV series Night Gallery, while George Lucas (born in nineteen forty-four) was working on his debut feature, THX eleven thirty-eight.
