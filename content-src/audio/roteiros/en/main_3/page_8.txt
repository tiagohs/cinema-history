## [01] The Wild One  (977 car., ~1.0 min)

Between the release of The Wild One, from nineteen fifty-three, and Easy Rider, from nineteen sixty-nine, Hollywood entered into a long and troubled relationship with American youth, explaining, indulging and exploiting the phenomenon of adolescence. Although important in their day, these teen-oriented films are by definition ephemeral, and only a few managed to make the theme of the mid-twentieth-century generation gap endure.

The Wild One, directed by László Benedek (nineteen oh-five to nineteen ninety-two), is generally considered the starting point of this phase, although it is only tangentially aimed at a teenage audience; the film's bikers seem more like outlaw cowboys than rebellious teenagers. Marlon Brando (nineteen twenty-four to two thousand four) was almost thirty when he made the film, but his inarticulate grace, along with his sunglasses, his leather jacket and his air of vague superiority, made him, for a brief time, the symbol of disaffected youth.

## [02] Learn more: Biker Movies  (955 car., ~0.9 min)

Learn more: Biker Movies.

Several productions followed The Wild One in the nineteen nineties, among them Motorcycle Gang, from nineteen fifty-seven, and the underground film Scorpio Rising, from nineteen sixty-four, directed by Kenneth Anger (born in nineteen twenty-seven). But biker movies only emerged as a subgenre after the success of Roger Corman's The Wild Angels, from nineteen sixty-six. Variations featured female gangs in The Mini-Skirt Mob, from nineteen sixty-eight, and bikers in The Losers, from nineteen seventy; there was also the self-explanatory Werewolves on Wheels, from nineteen seventy-one. The Wild Angels also inspired Easy Rider, from nineteen sixty-nine, a more artistic film that gave rise to the road movies of the nineteen seventies, including Electra Glide in Blue, from nineteen seventy-three. Biker films remain nostalgic; the formula also gave rise to the TV series Sons of Anarchy (two thousand eight to twenty fourteen).

## [03] James Dean  (805 car., ~0.8 min)

James Dean (nineteen thirty-one to nineteen fifty-five) replaced Brando's intermittent intensity with an introspective hurt. In his first major film, East of Eden, from nineteen fifty-five, directed by Elia Kazan (nineteen oh-nine to two thousand three), Dean's portrayal of an unstable son fighting for his father's love hit young audiences of the mid-nineteen fifties hard. Released the same year, the urban drama Rebel Without a Cause, from nineteen fifty-five, directed by Nicholas Ray (nineteen eleven to nineteen seventy-nine), took the world of juvenile delinquency off the streets and brought it into a prosperous middle-class neighborhood. The film also gave Dean the role with which he is most identified. His Jim Stark is not so much a rebel; he simply does not understand the world around him.

## [04] Blackboard Jungle  (825 car., ~0.8 min)

In Blackboard Jungle, from nineteen fifty-five, Richard Brooks (nineteen twelve to nineteen ninety-two) brings juvenile delinquency back into the inner-city school system. With a touch of well-meaning social criticism, and Glenn Ford as a teacher forced to confront his delinquent students, the film was overshadowed by its inclusion of Bill Haley's song "Rock Around the Clock".

It was the first major Hollywood film to use rock music on its soundtrack, and in British cinemas teenagers caused disturbances at screenings of the film. In the nineteen fifties, rock, not cinema, was the ultimate expression of youth experience, with the young Elvis Presley (nineteen thirty-five to nineteen seventy-seven) as its charismatic god.

## [05] Jailhouse Rock  (1093 car., ~1.1 min)

Presley's film career began with dramatic roles; Jailhouse Rock, from nineteen fifty-seven, was one of the few films to capture the rawness of his early years. After his Army service, his acting career went into decline, with a string of good-guy roles in increasingly tedious musicals.

Too amorphous to be considered a genre, these nineteen fifties films seem to show that Hollywood was trying to understand and exploit both the new generation and the discontents of youth. James Dean's brief career produced one of the definitive portrayals of America's mid-twentieth-century rebel generation. His death, before the release of Rebel Without a Cause, forever fixed his image as an anguished, lost teenager.

By the mid-nineteen sixties, the emerging counterculture had changed productions aimed at teenagers. In nineteen sixty-six, Roger Corman (born in nineteen twenty-six) directed biker films such as The Wild Angels, from nineteen sixty-six. Featuring a gang of rapists and a biker's funeral with a coffin draped in a Nazi flag, The Wild Angels is far more confrontational than The Wild One.
