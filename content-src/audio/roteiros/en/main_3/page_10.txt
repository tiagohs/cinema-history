## [01] Michael Curtiz  (2134 car., ~2.1 min)

Michael Curtiz's (eighteen eighty-six to nineteen sixty-two) Yankee Doodle Dandy, from nineteen forty-two, was in tune with the mood of a nation entering World War Two, being patriotic, sentimental, aggressive and almost a deification of President Franklin D. Roosevelt, as were its pretentious subject and the songs and choreography of George M. Cohan (James Cagney). The closing of markets in war-ravaged Europe forced Hollywood to seek foreign income in Latin America, which led to a mini boom of Latin American musicals such as Down Argentine Way, from nineteen forty, starring Carmen Miranda (nineteen oh-nine to nineteen fifty-five).

The general tone was one of exaggerated cheer in the star-studded cast of Thank Your Lucky Stars, from nineteen forty-three. By contrast, Meet Me in St. Louis, from nineteen forty-four, by Vincente Minnelli (nineteen oh-three to nineteen eighty-six) and starring Judy Garland (nineteen twenty-two to nineteen sixty-nine), was a nostalgic, bittersweet film that revolved around old songs, looking back to the turn of the twentieth century.

After the war, biopics of singers such as Al Jolson (Larry Parks) in The Jolson Story, from nineteen forty-six, or of composers such as Cole Porter (Cary Grant) in Night and Day, from nineteen forty-six, continued to prevail. Heavily fictionalized and artificial, this cycle relied on time-honored clichés.

Fred Astaire and Ginger Rogers, the great musical stars of the nineteen thirties, reunited for one last, weak film, The Barkleys of Broadway, from nineteen forty-nine, but Astaire was much more at ease in the company of singer Bing Crosby in films such as Holiday Inn, from nineteen forty-two. His standing as the leading name in musicals was being threatened by the younger and obviously more athletic Gene Kelly (nineteen twelve to nineteen ninety-six), who debuted with For Me and My Gal, from nineteen forty-two, and was soon creating innovative works such as Anchors Aweigh, from nineteen forty-five, in which he dances with the cartoon star MGM's Jerry Mouse, and Cover Girl, from nineteen forty-four, in which he dances with his own reflection.

Directed by Gene Kelly and Stanley Donen (born in nineteen twenty-four) and inspired by Leonard Bernstein's nineteen forty-four Broadway show, the songwriting team of Bernstein, Adolph Green and Betty Comden worked with Roger Edens on several numbers for the film.

## [05] The Band Wagon  (1021 car., ~1.0 min)

For example, the motto of Kelly's vain movie star is "Dignity, always dignity", inspired by the tumbling of his vaudeville shows early in his career. Interestingly, this became the theme of Minnelli's answer film, the innovative, risqué and divinely creative backstage musical The Band Wagon, from nineteen fifty-three. Astaire, whose contribution to artificial choreography had been the dance on the ceiling in Royal Wedding, from nineteen fifty-one, plays musical star Tony Hunter, who hears jokes about his age for much of the film and is paired with a ballerina played by Cyd Charisse, in the first of her spectacular performances.

With Kelly, Donen made It's Always Fair Weather, from nineteen fifty-five, a disillusioned, though interesting, follow-up to On the Town.

## [06] Fred Zinnemann  (1064 car., ~1.1 min)

The Broadway hit Oklahoma!, from nineteen forty-three, was the emblematic musical of its time, but it only reached the screen in nineteen fifty-five. Directed by Fred Zinnemann (nineteen oh-seven to nineteen ninety-seven), its long run encouraged Western musicals off the singing-cowboy trail, but its importance lies in confirming the direct link between Broadway and Hollywood, which kept up a flow of stage-to-screen adaptations throughout the decade, among them Show Boat, from nineteen fifty-one, Carmen Jones, from nineteen fifty-four, Guys and Dolls, from nineteen fifty-five, The King and I, from nineteen fifty-six, South Pacific, from nineteen fifty-eight, and Porgy and Bess, from nineteen fifty-nine.

Some of them allowed film and music stars such as Doris Day and Sinatra to take on celebrated roles with great songs; others allowed stage actors such as Yul Brynner to repeat the great performances of their careers; and still others surprisingly cast Rossano Brazzi as a French aristocrat, or Marlon Brando, who was no singer, as the lead in musicals.

## [07] Marilyn Monroe  (1369 car., ~1.4 min)

The nineteen fifties created old-fashioned showcases for talents such as Marilyn Monroe (nineteen twenty-six to nineteen sixty-two) and Jane Russell in Howard Hawks's (eighteen ninety-six to nineteen seventy-seven) Gentlemen Prefer Blondes, from nineteen fifty-three, or Crosby and Danny Kaye in Curtiz's White Christmas, from nineteen fifty-four. Crosby teamed up with Sinatra and Grace Kelly in High Society, from nineteen fifty-six, a remake of The Philadelphia Story, from nineteen forty.

In nineteen fifty-six, Elvis Presley made his film debut with Love Me Tender, from nineteen fifty-six. The musicals that followed were built around his sexualized, simple-hearted presence, but his potential was sabotaged by poor career management. Frank Tashlin's (nineteen thirteen to nineteen seventy-two) The Girl Can't Help It, from nineteen fifty-six, was the first great pop musical, with appearances by attractive young artists such as Eddie Cochran (nineteen thirty-eight to nineteen sixty), Little Richard and Fats Domino.

But Richard Lester (born in nineteen thirty-two) would end up changing the game with A Hard Day's Night, from nineteen sixty-four. Using creative song montages instead of revered performances, Lester started a process through which the movie musical would eventually be replaced by music videos, turned into something secondary to a music career.
